[Mistral AI](/en/articles/mistral) and [DeepSeek](/en/articles/deepseek) have two things in common. Their operating companies are outside the United States (Mistral in Paris, DeepSeek in Hangzhou). And both publish the weights of their main models for anyone to download. If a company gives the weights away, what does it sell? Put the two anatomy articles on top of each other and the answers point in opposite directions.

This piece is based on a re-read of both companies' official pages, terms and model cards on 2 October 2026. This site has not run either company's models, so it does not compare performance, and it does not rank one above the other.

## The weights they release, and what the licence texts say

"Open weights" is one phrase, but the licences are not the same.

:::fact
Mistral's official model list (checked 2 October 2026) labels Mistral Large 3 and Mistral Small 4 "Apache 2.0" and Mistral Medium 3.5 "Modified MIT". The licence field of the Hugging Face repositories for Large 3 and Small 4 also read apache-2.0. The LICENSE file of Medium 3.5 adds one condition to the MIT text: you are not authorised to exercise any rights under the licence if the global consolidated monthly revenue of your company (or your employer) exceeded $20 million in the preceding month. In that case the file points to requesting a commercial licence from Mistral or using Mistral's hosted services. The restriction is stated to apply to derivatives as well. The same list also has models labelled "Premier" (OCR 4.1, Codestral and others) and "CC BY-NC 4.0" (Voxtral TTS).
:::

:::fact
DeepSeek's weights are published on Hugging Face (checked 2 October 2026). DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and DeepSeek-V4-Pro-0813 both show mit in the licence field, and their LICENSE files contain the standard MIT text. The model card says the repository and the model weights are licensed under the MIT License. The LICENSE file contains no condition based on revenue.
:::

| Item | Mistral | DeepSeek |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main current open-weight models | Mistral Medium 3.5, Mistral Large 3, Mistral Small 4 | DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, DeepSeek-V4-Pro |
| Licence | Modified MIT for Medium 3.5; Apache 2.0 for Large 3 and Small 4 | MIT for both |
| Condition on the user in the licence text | Medium 3.5 only: companies above $20 million in monthly revenue may not exercise the rights | No revenue condition in the LICENSE file |
| Models without published weights | Yes (labelled "Premier" in the list) | Both current API models have published weights |

The FAQ on Mistral's pricing page has one more sentence worth reading. After answering that you can self-host the models anywhere, it continues: open-weight models (for example Mistral 7B) are Apache 2.0 licensed for research and individual use, while commercial deployments require a Mistral licence with separate terms for derivatives and production use. The Apache 2.0 text itself permits commercial use, and this site has not been able to confirm how that FAQ wording maps onto the licence labels in the model list. For commercial use, reading the LICENSE file of each model directly is the reliable route.

:::guess
Putting a revenue ceiling only on the newest flagship model appears to work as a route that sends large companies to Mistral's hosted services or a commercial licence. It reads as a line drawn so that the model spreads for free among small companies and researchers while larger companies pay. DeepSeek keeping an unconditional MIT licence suggests that it is not designed to earn from the weights themselves. Neither company has explained its intent officially, and other readings are possible.
:::

## The API price lists, side by side

Next, the list prices of the APIs that each company runs and sells itself. All figures are US dollars per 1 million tokens, as displayed on each company's pricing page on 2 October 2026.

| Model (API) | Input | Cached input | Output | Conditions as displayed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mistral Medium 3.5 | 1.5 | 90% off input tokens (as shown on the pricing page toggle; the FAQ says "up to 90%") | 7.5 | Standard list price. Batch is half price. Regional inference is +10% |
| Mistral Large 3 | 0.5 | Same | 1.5 | Same |
| Mistral Small 4 | 0.15 | Same | 0.6 | Same |
| deepseek-flash (DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash), peak | 0.3 (cache miss) | 0.006 (cache hit) | 1.2 | Peak is 01:00-04:00 and 06:00-10:00 UTC, Monday to Friday, excluding Chinese public holidays |
| deepseek-flash, off-peak | 0.15 (cache miss) | 0.003 (cache hit) | 0.6 | All other hours, including weekends and Chinese public holidays in full |
| deepseek-v4-pro (DeepSeek-V4-Pro-0813), peak | 1.32 (cache miss) | 0.044 (cache hit) | 3.96 | Same peak definition |
| deepseek-v4-pro, off-peak | 0.66 (cache miss) | 0.022 (cache hit) | 1.98 | Same off-peak definition |

:::fact
Where the table comes from, and the conditions that did not fit in it. The Mistral figures come from the official API pricing page. The same page shows Regional inference as "+10%", cached input tokens as "-90%", and Batch at half price. The official documentation describes regional inference as billed at 1.1x standard list pricing for input tokens, output tokens, cached reads and cache writes. Enterprise APIs (regional data processing controls, system-level SLAs, increased rate limits and premium support) are described as available for 75% above list pricing on select APIs. The DeepSeek figures come from the official Models & Pricing page. Its note says off-peak rates are half of the peak rates, defines peak hours as 01:00-04:00 and 06:00-10:00 UTC, Monday through Friday, excluding Chinese public holidays, and treats all other hours as off-peak. The caching guide says context caching is enabled by default for all users, and that the part of a request whose prefix overlaps a previous request counts as a cache hit.
:::

From here on, the arithmetic is this site's own. It looks only at the effect of caching within the same model of the same company. For deepseek-flash at peak, $0.006 for a cache hit divided by $0.3 for a cache miss is 0.02, so cached input costs one fiftieth of uncached input. For deepseek-v4-pro, 0.044 / 1.32 is about 0.033, roughly one thirtieth. For Mistral, a 90% discount as displayed means one tenth. Applied to Medium 3.5 that would be 1.5 x 0.1 = $0.15, but that is this site's calculation, not a figure shown on the pricing page.

This site does not calculate an "X times cheaper" figure across the two companies. The models differ in size and in what they are built for, and this site has not checked whether they do the same job at the same quality. Two things can be said from the numbers alone. The list price of Mistral Small 4 ($0.15 input, $0.6 output) and the off-peak, cache-miss price of deepseek-flash ($0.15 input, $0.6 output) are the same figures. And the lowest price for cached input in the table is in the DeepSeek rows.

:::pull
Mistral charges 1.1x when you pick a region. DeepSeek charges half when you avoid peak hours. The dials on the two price lists are attached in different places.
:::

:::guess
What the price lists show appears to be less a gap in list prices than a difference in which direction discounts and surcharges run. DeepSeek attaches large discounts to resending the same context and to moving non-urgent work to less busy hours. As the [DeepSeek](/en/articles/deepseek) article notes, the company puts cache compression at the centre of the V4.1-Flash design, so the discount is explained on the engineering side. Mistral instead prices the items that sit on top of list price: region selection, SLAs and priority capacity. The two lists seem to address different buyers, one looking for a low unit price and one looking for conditions.
:::

## For individuals: a subscription on one side, a free app on the other

The entry points for individuals also differ in shape.

:::fact
Mistral's pricing page (checked 2 October 2026) shows four tiers for its agent Vibe: Free, Pro ($14.99 per month, excluding taxes), Team ($24.99 per user per month, excluding taxes) and Enterprise (contact sales). The FAQ says verified students can get Pro for $5.99 per month. The Free plan is listed with $10 per month in API credits. Usage beyond a plan's limit can continue on pay-as-you-go credits at API rates. The Enterprise column lists "Custom deployments": self-hosted, in a private cloud, or on-premises.
:::

:::fact
The download page on DeepSeek's official site (checked the same day) describes the app as a way to chat with leading AI models for free and offers iOS and Android versions. The same page lists DeepSeek Harness, a desktop agent environment for macOS and Windows. As far as this site could see, the official site's navigation does not present a monthly plan, and prices appear on the API pricing page. Numeric usage limits for the free app could not be confirmed from the official pages.
:::

:::guess
Mistral appears to connect a consumer subscription and the developer API inside one contract and to build a staircase from there up to enterprise agreements. DeepSeek appears to treat the consumer side as a free entry point and to concentrate charging on pay-as-you-go API usage. Which shape suits a user depends on whether they want to buy a monthly allowance or pay only for what they use.
:::

## Where data is processed, and which law applies

This is where the two companies' documents differ most clearly. The table below puts what each company's own terms, privacy policy and official documentation say under the same headings. No legal assessment is added.

| Item | Mistral (as stated in its documents) | DeepSeek (as stated in its documents) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operating entity | A French company incorporated in Paris (privacy policy) | Hangzhou DeepSeek Artificial Intelligence Co., Ltd., with its registered address in China (privacy policy) |
| Place of processing and storage | The API has three endpoints. Global: no commitment to a specific inference location. EU: multiple data centres in EU and EFTA countries. US: multiple data centres in the United States. Account configuration, API keys, billing, usage analytics and similar data may be handled outside the selected region (official documentation) | Information collected is stored on secure servers located in the People's Republic of China. It may be stored on a server outside the country where the user lives (privacy policy) |
| Transfers across borders | Providers within the EU are prioritised; where a non-EU provider is used in exceptional cases, safeguards under Article 46 GDPR and Standard Contractual Clauses are attached (privacy policy) | Where required, appropriate safeguards are used for transferring personal information outside certain countries, in line with applicable data protection laws (privacy policy) |
| Governing law and courts | Depends on where the user is located. Americas: California law and the courts of Santa Clara County. Asia-Pacific, including Japan: the laws of Singapore and the courts of Singapore. Elsewhere: French law and the courts of Paris. EEA consumers: the courts and law of their country of residence, or the courts of Paris and French law (consumer terms and Commercial Terms) | The laws of the People's Republic of China in the mainland. If negotiation fails, a court with jurisdiction over the registered office of Hangzhou DeepSeek Artificial Intelligence Co., Ltd. (Terms of Use section 9, Open Platform Terms section 10) |
| Retention | Vibe: until the account or the conversation is deleted. API: the period needed to generate the output plus 30 days for abuse monitoring. On paid plans, zero data retention can be requested for supported API endpoints (privacy policy and official documentation) | As long as necessary to provide the services and for the other purposes in the policy. Information processed to provide the service is kept for as long as the user has an account (privacy policy) |
| Use for training | Input and output are used to train models unless the user opts out, which can be done in account settings. The commercial terms say customer data is not used for training except in listed cases (privacy policy and terms) | Inputs and outputs may be used, to the minimum extent and on the premise of anonymisation, to improve the services. Users have a right to refuse the use of personal data for model training, exercised by email (Terms of Use 4.3 and privacy policy) |

:::fact
A further note on Mistral's documents. The official documentation states that regional inference does not make the control plane regional, and that stateful features (Agents, Batch and the Files API) are not available on regional endpoints. The announcement of 11 August 2026 notes that limited, safeguarded transfers to sub-processors outside the selected region may occur. The privacy policy names the French data protection authority (CNIL) as a place to lodge a complaint.
:::

:::fact
A further note on DeepSeek's documents. On 3 February 2025 (updated 5 March 2025), Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission published an information notice about what the company's published privacy policy states. It makes two points: data including personal information acquired by the company is stored on servers located in the People's Republic of China, and the laws of the People's Republic of China apply to that data. The same notice lists examples of applicable laws for reference. This site has not checked whether the Commission has published a notice of the same kind about Mistral.
:::

With either company there is also a route that does not use the official API or app: running the published weights in your own environment or on a third party's cloud. In that case the party running the model decides where data is processed. Note that Mistral Medium 3.5 carries the revenue condition described above, and that a third-party cloud's own terms apply separately.

:::guess
What Mistral sells under the word "sovereignty" appears, on paper, to be the choice of inference location, SLAs and support for deployment in the customer's own environment. As the [Mistral AI](/en/articles/mistral) article notes, the company says most of its customers already run its models inside their own data centres and cloud environments. In DeepSeek's official services, as far as the documents show, there is no setting for choosing a processing region. For users who want to choose the location, the MIT-licensed weights themselves seem to be the answer. One side makes the choice of location a product; the other leaves that choice to the published weights. Which fits a given use depends on the nature of the data and on the rules of the organisation involved.
:::

## Ownership and funding: disclosed in different ways

:::fact
Mistral announces its funding rounds itself. According to its announcement dated 9 September 2025, the Series C was EUR 1.7 billion at a post-money valuation of EUR 11.7 billion, led by ASML. According to its announcement dated 8 September 2026, the Series D was EUR 3 billion at a post-money valuation of more than EUR 21 billion. Samsung Electronics led the round, with Scaleup Europe Fund (managed by EQT) and existing investor PSG Equity as co-leads. Advent, funds and accounts managed by BlackRock, and the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg are named as new investors, and existing investors including Bpifrance, NVIDIA and a16z are listed as participating. The same announcement says the company operates across 20 countries and supports more than 125 enterprises. On compute, the official AI Cloud page lists GB200, GB300 and B300 GPUs and sets a capacity target of 1 GW across the EU by 2030.
:::

:::fact
On DeepSeek's official site, as far as this site could see, there is no page describing investors or funding. What follows is from a secondary source. According to Wikipedia's summary, the Chinese hedge fund High-Flyer announced an AGI research lab on 14 April 2023, and that lab became an independent company on 17 July 2023. The company is described as owned and funded by High-Flyer, with co-founder Liang Wenfeng as CEO. The same source says the company completed a Series A of US$7 billion in May 2026 at a post-money valuation of US$52 billion, and that Bloomberg and the Financial Times reported in July 2026 that it had begun preparing for an IPO. This site has not been able to confirm these in an official announcement by the company. What could be confirmed in a primary source is a line in the official V4.1-Flash news post inviting enquiries about large-scale deployments with 2,000 GPUs and a storage cluster. The scale and location of the compute used for training and inference could not be confirmed from the official documentation.
:::

:::guess
Announcing funding in its own words and listing its investors appears to be part of how Mistral presents itself to enterprise and government buyers as a supplier that will be around for the long term. DeepSeek publishes technical and price information in detail, through model cards, technical reports and the price list, while saying less officially about the company itself. This is not a matter of one being better; it reads as a difference in what each chooses to disclose in order to earn trust. Neither company publishes its revenue.
:::

## The tech stacks overlap in exactly one place: Next.js

The tech-stack comparison shown below this article is produced mechanically from the techStack data of the two articles. The only item it reports as shared is Next.js. Even that is an observation on Mistral's documentation site and on DeepSeek's official site, both inferred from response headers and HTML (confidence: likely). The models, the compute and the delivery networks do not overlap. On the Mistral side the list has Mistral Medium 3.5, Large 3 and Small 4, Mistral Compute, Regional Endpoints, Priority Tier, Kong, Cloudflare, Astro, Netlify and Vercel. On the DeepSeek side it has DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, APIs compatible with the OpenAI and Anthropic formats, DeepSeek Harness, Amazon CloudFront, Amazon S3, AWS WAF, Docusaurus, Tencent Cloud and others.

That diff points the same way as the rest of this piece. The Mistral side lists parts for "where it runs and under what guarantee". The DeepSeek side lists an API that can be called in other vendors' formats and an agent environment that runs on the user's own machine: parts that lower the effort of getting started.

From the same starting point of giving the weights away, [Mistral AI](/en/articles/mistral) put a price on where the model runs and on guarantees, and [DeepSeek](/en/articles/deepseek) put a price on cache hits and hours of the day. On the price lists, what moves the number is region selection and SLAs for the former, and cache hits and off-peak hours for the latter. For someone choosing, the first thing to settle is probably not the size of the unit price but the conditions: where the data is processed and under which law it sits. After that comes reading both companies' documents against your own use.
