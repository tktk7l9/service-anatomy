[beehiiv](/en/articles/beehiiv) and [Mailchimp](/en/articles/mailchimp) are often grouped together as services for sending bulk email. Put the price lists side by side, though, and they sell different things. beehiiv's list features an ad network, recommendations and a 0% take on subscriptions. Mailchimp's features automation flows, segments, seats and onboarding. This article reads both companies' official pages on November 2, 2026 and lines up the numbers and conditions they state. We have not subscribed to either service; we are comparing public price lists and documentation. Prices change, so always check the official pages before signing up.

## Billing units and free tiers

:::fact
beehiiv's Markdown pricing page says price depends on subscriber tiers, in US dollars. Free costs $0 for up to 2,500 subscribers, and sends are unlimited on every plan. Lite is sold through 100,000 and Pro through the 250,000 tier, with Enterprise beyond that and for custom terms. Annual billing has a per-month equivalent: at 1,000 subscribers Lite is $59 a month, or $49 a month billed annually.

Mailchimp's pricing data uses JPY as the base when viewed from Japan, and notes that USD figures are converted estimates. Displayed prices exclude VAT. Free allows 250 contacts, 500 sends a month and 250 a day. Paid send limits are 10 to 15 times the contact limit. The help center says subscribed, unsubscribed and non-subscribed contacts all count, while archived, cleaned and deleted contacts do not. Past the limit, Free stops sending and paid plans continue, billing add-on blocks.

The table sets them at the same scale. Note that subscribers and contacts are not the same thing. beehiiv figures are monthly billing (annual per-month equivalent in parentheses). Mailchimp figures are the monthly price in the pricing data, with the USD estimate in parentheses.

| Scale | beehiiv Lite | beehiiv Pro | Mailchimp Essentials | Mailchimp Standard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2,500 | $79 ($69) | $149 ($129) | ¥3,800 ($23.94) | ¥6,300 ($39.69) |
| 10,000 | $139 ($119) | $239 ($199) | ¥9,500 ($59.86) | ¥13,000 ($81.91) |
| 50,000 | $299 ($249) | $469 ($399) | ¥33,000 ($207.92) | ¥38,000 ($239.43) |
| 100,000 | $469 ($399) | $699 ($599) | Not offered | ¥63,000 ($396.94) |

On Standard, Mailchimp's send limit at those scales is 120,000 a month at 10,000 contacts and 1.2 million at 100,000. Up to 2,500, beehiiv's Free costs $0, while Mailchimp needs a paid plan from the 251st contact.
:::

:::guess
The gap in free tiers likely reflects what each company expects from free users. beehiiv's 2,500 looks like a runway for writers to build an audience and start earning. Mailchimp's 250 reads as a place to try features, assuming working businesses move to paid soon. Meanwhile, within the table, Mailchimp's USD estimates are lower, with Standard and beehiiv Lite's annual price almost level at 100,000. But the 12-times limit can matter if you send often. Two issues a week is about 9 sends a month and fits inside 12 times, but three a week, about 13, to the whole list goes slightly over if you are at your contact limit. beehiiv's unlimited sends likely favor high-frequency publications.
:::

:::pull
beehiiv's meter runs on readers. Mailchimp's runs on both contacts and sends. For a publication that sends often and a shop that sends selectively, the same 10,000 means a different bill.
:::

## Earning, and moving customers

:::fact
beehiiv's pricing FAQ says it takes 0% of paid subscription revenue, and writers keep what they earn minus Stripe's standard fee of 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction. Lite adds the ad network, recommendations, 0% on paid subscriptions, digital products, community and email automations. The ad network and recommendations are marked as not in trial and not in all regions, and the Ad Network FAQ says payouts require Stripe Express, with no PayPal or Wise.

In Mailchimp's comparison table, Free has no automation flows, Essentials allows up to 4 steps, and Standard and Premium allow up to 200 flows. Standard and up add sending custom-coded templates, generative AI features, multivariate testing and predictive segments, and Premium adds phone support and enterprise SSO. SMS is a paid-plan add-on for the US, Canada, European countries and Australia. The Mailchimp pricing and help pages we read did not describe a feature for charging subscriptions or a network that pays publishers for ads.

Seats are 1, 2, 10 and unlimited on beehiiv, and 1, 3, 5 and unlimited on Mailchimp. Removing branding starts at Pro on beehiiv and Essentials on Mailchimp. Support on beehiiv Free is the beehiiv Agent, with humans from Lite. On Mailchimp Free it is email for the first 30 days, with 24/7 email and chat from Essentials.
:::

:::guess
beehiiv's add-ons appear to extend toward turning readers into income, and Mailchimp's toward reacting to customer behavior with the next touchpoint. For e-commerce tactics such as abandoned-cart or repurchase prompts, Mailchimp with its integrations and flows is the natural fit. For a publication whose product is its readers, much of Mailchimp goes unused, and with no ad brokerage, income is yours to build.
:::

## Delivery safeguards and sending infrastructure

:::fact
beehiiv's sub-processor list names SendGrid for email delivery. Its pricing page includes "Optimized Deliverability" from Free and places dedicated IPs in Enterprise. Deliverability reports are marked "coming soon".

Mailchimp's Omnivore checks addresses at import or when sending to new people and, if it predicts high bounce or complaint risk, disables sending to that audience. The help center says Gmail and Yahoo require custom authentication and DMARC from senders above 5,000 emails in 24 hours, and recommends Entri to automate the DNS setup. Transactional, for developers, has per-account reputation and an hourly quota, and offers a dedicated IP as a $29.95 monthly add-on. The docs say it is worth considering at 5,000 emails a day on at least three days a week, and can hurt at low volume.
:::

:::guess
On either service, delivery likely rests on shared infrastructure plus your own authentication and list quality. We could not find public delivery figures that allow a fair comparison, so we make no judgment on which reaches inboxes better. DKIM and DMARC on your own domain come first whichever you choose.
:::

## Developer and AI access

:::fact
The Marketing API allows 10 simultaneous connections per user and has a 120-second timeout. Transactional (formerly Mandrill) sends individual messages such as order confirmations and password resets by API or SMTP, sold from $20 per block of 25,000. It requires Standard or higher, and Essentials is not eligible. beehiiv offers its API from Free (excluding Send API), with Send API and AI bot control from Pro and webhooks from Lite.

The AI entrances are designed differently. beehiiv's MCP is read-only on Free, can create and edit on Lite, and can schedule and send on Pro and Enterprise. Mailchimp's docs MCP only searches published documentation and explicitly cannot act on your account. Transactional has an MCP that can call the API and diagnose failed sends, and on the marketing side an app inside ChatGPT and Claude drafts campaigns that you launch in Mailchimp.
:::

:::guess
Sending payment confirmations or password resets from an app has different requirements from a newsletter, and Mailchimp is the one with a product for it on the price list. But Transactional needs a Standard or higher plan, so if that is all you want, specialist delivery services are likely worth comparing too. On AI, beehiiv opens up actions inside the account, while Mailchimp's marketing side appears to keep a human check in the loop.
:::

## Which fits whom

From here on is our reading of the price lists and conditions above. It does not rank the services; it shows which rows matter for each use.

:::guess
- Individual writers and media that want to grow readers and earn from subscriptions or ads likely fit beehiiv: free up to 2,500, no send limit, and a 0% take on subscriptions. Payouts from ads and recommendations assume Stripe Express, with regional limits.
- Online and physical shops that want automatic emails triggered by purchases or visits likely fit Mailchimp. Standard's flows and segments, SMS (in supported countries) and Transactional sit in one account.
- A small school or local shop sending one notice a month can use either Free plan at 250 people or fewer, and beehiiv's Free up to 2,500. Mailchimp also has Pay As You Go for infrequent senders.
- High-frequency publishing, close to daily, likely favors beehiiv's unlimited sends. On Mailchimp you need to factor in send limits and add-on blocks.
- If you need everything in Japanese, Mailchimp's language table does not list it, and we read beehiiv's pricing and help pages in English too. It is safest to assume English screens and support for both.
:::

## Why the designs differ

:::fact
According to [our beehiiv article](/en/articles/beehiiv), beehiiv was founded in 2021 by early Morning Brew staff, and its About page cites over 170,000 publications and 129 employees. According to [our Mailchimp article](/en/articles/mailchimp), Mailchimp began in 2001 as an email service for small businesses and was bought by Intuit in 2021 for about $12 billion.
:::

:::guess
The difference likely comes from who the first customers were. beehiiv's were writers whose audience was the business itself, so price follows reader count, sends are unlimited, and income tools are stacked on top. Mailchimp's were small businesses with other work to do, so email was one customer touchpoint. It grew toward storing customer data and reacting automatically, and made the contact the billing unit.

The two are moving into each other's territory. beehiiv has added automation and APIs, and Mailchimp uses AI to lighten content creation. The billing units stay put, likely because each product was shaped around its original customer.
:::

beehiiv and Mailchimp have you send the same email for different ends. What matters is less which monthly price is lower than what you intend that email to earn. If readers are the asset, beehiiv; if customer behavior is the trigger, Mailchimp. Once that is settled, it is clear which rows of the two price lists to read.
