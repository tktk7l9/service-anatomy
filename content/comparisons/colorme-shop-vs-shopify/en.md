[ColorMe Shop](/en/articles/colorme-shop) and [Shopify](/en/articles/shopify) are both services for building an online shop on your own domain. When an individual or a small business in Japan opening a first shop compares the two, the monthly fee is the first number they see. But reading the official pages side by side, something differs before the monthly fee does: where on the price list "the things you need to sell in Japan" are placed.

This article is written only from what could be confirmed on both companies' official pages on October 1, 2026. This site has not signed up for or used either service, and it does not rate how they feel to use. Prices and terms change, so check the official pages before you sign up.

## The prices side by side (as of October 1, 2026)

:::fact
According to ColorMe Shop's official pricing page, there are four plans — Free, Regular, Large and Premium — and the page notes that "all prices include tax" (Japan's consumption tax). The sales commission is 0 yen on all three paid plans. Paid plans are contracted for 3, 6 or 12 months. Regular and Large have a 30-day free trial. A shop can move from the Free plan to Regular or above from the admin screen, but cannot move from Regular or above back to the Free plan.
:::

| ColorMe Shop | Monthly fee | Initial fee | Card processing fee |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free | 0 yen | 0 yen | from 6.6% + 30 yen |
| Regular | 4,950 yen | 3,300 yen | from 3.4% |
| Large | 9,595 yen | 3,300 yen | from 3.19% |
| Premium | from 35,640 yen (the comparison table's monthly-fee row says 39,600 yen) | 22,000 yen | from 2.99% |

:::fact
According to the notes on the same page, using a payment service requires a separate contract with a payment processor, and the processing fees in the table are based on the rates under the payment package "ColorMe Payment." The Free plan page writes the processing fee as "from 6.6% + 30 yen" in its table and as "6.6% of the payment amount + 33 yen" in its FAQ. The Premium plan page gives the monthly fee as "from 35,640 yen" and explains that longer contracts lower the monthly fee.
:::

:::fact
According to an official notice (2026-09-09), from November 1, 2026 the Regular plan goes from 4,950 yen to 5,940 yen a month and the Large plan from 9,595 yen to 11,000 yen. A contract that starts before November 1 is made at the pre-revision price. For someone signing up now, the monthly fee depends on which side of that date the contract starts.
:::

:::fact
According to Shopify's pricing page for Japan, there are four plans: Basic, Grow, Advanced and Plus. The page says the first 3 days are free and the following 3 months cost 150 yen a month; its FAQ explains that no credit card is needed for the free trial and that "most plans" cost 150 yen a month for the first 3 months after the trial. The same FAQ says there is no setup fee on any plan. No statement of whether prices include or exclude tax was found on this page.
:::

| Shopify | Paid monthly | Paid annually (per month) | Shopify Payments domestic card rate | Third-party transaction fee |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Basic | 4,850 yen | 3,650 yen | 3.55% + 0 yen | 2% |
| Grow | 13,500 yen | 10,100 yen | 3.4% + 0 yen | 1% |
| Advanced | 58,500 yen | 44,000 yen | 3.25% + 0 yen | 0.6% |
| Plus | from 368,000 yen | not listed | 2.9% + 0 yen | 0.2% |

:::fact
According to the same page, the domestic card rate in the table is shared by the "standard online card rate" and the "JCB online credit card fee," while the rate for online Amex cards and international cards is 3.9% on Basic, 3.85% on Grow, 3.8% on Advanced and 3.75% on Plus. The FAQ explains that a third-party transaction fee may apply when a shop uses an external payment service instead of Shopify Payments, and that the rate depends on the plan.
:::

The two tables cannot be compared as they stand. ColorMe Shop states that its prices include tax; no tax statement was found on Shopify's page. ColorMe Shop's paid plans are contracted for three months or more, while Shopify offers monthly or annual payment. ColorMe Shop's card rates are "from" figures for a payment service contracted separately. The numbers have to be read with those conditions lined up.

## Where Japan's payment methods sit

This is where the practical difference shows. Japan has payment methods that shoppers use widely besides cards: konbini payment (paying in cash at a convenience store), bank transfer, carrier billing (the charge is added to the shopper's mobile phone bill) and deferred payment (atobarai: the shopper pays from an invoice after the goods arrive). The two services put these in different places.

:::fact
According to its official page, ColorMe Payment is a payment package provided by GMO Epsilon, and its fees are paid to GMO Epsilon rather than to ColorMe Shop (GMO Pepabo). The page's price table (tax included, for shops on a paid plan) lists Amazon Pay at 3.9%, PayPay at 3.45% (plus 2,200 yen a month on Regular and Large), Rakuten Pay at 4% (plus 2,200 yen a month), konbini payment from 165 yen per transaction, online-bank payment, cash on delivery from 352 yen per transaction, smartphone carrier billing at 6% (3,300 yen a month for the three-carrier set), GMO deferred payment at 4.0% + 279 yen per transaction (1,100 yen a month), and bank transfer to a virtual account at 22 yen per transaction (1,100 yen a month). In the table for corporations, konbini payment and some other methods carry a monthly minimum fee of 1,100 yen. Payouts are closed at month end and paid at the end of the following month; use requires a review, and it takes roughly 5 to 24 business days from application to start. The eligibility condition is having applied for or contracted a paid plan, and the page says shops on the Free plan are not eligible.
:::

:::fact
The same page has separate tables for corporations and individuals, and the table for individuals has no row for smartphone carrier billing. The carrier billing feature page also states that it cannot be used under an individual contract and cannot be used on the Free plan. In the table for individuals, card payment and konbini payment are marked "required for individual contracts."
:::

:::fact
According to Shopify's Help Center, the payment methods available with Shopify Payments in Japan are Visa, Mastercard, American Express and JCB cards (JCB for transactions in JPY only), Apple Pay, Google Pay and Shop Pay, plus local methods for European shoppers such as Bancontact and iDEAL. When this site checked, konbini payment, bank transfer, carrier billing and deferred payment were not on that list. Meanwhile, the data on Shopify's payment gateways page includes names such as KOMOJU's konbini payment, bank transfer, Pay-easy and Paidy; SB Payment Service's konbini payment, carrier billing, PayPay and Rakuten Pay; GMO Epsilon's konbini payment, smartphone carrier billing and GMO deferred payment; Paidy's deferred payment; and Amazon Pay.
:::

:::fact
According to the Help Center, third-party transaction fees apply to all third-party and alternative payment gateways even when Shopify Payments is activated. When Shopify Payments is activated, PayPal and manual payment methods are excluded. Manual payment methods are things like bank transfer and cash on delivery: the order is recorded as unpaid, and the merchant marks it as paid by hand after confirming the money has arrived.
:::

:::guess
Putting those two together, a Shopify shop that accepts konbini payment or carrier billing through an external payment service would appear to pay that service's own fee plus the third-party transaction fee — 2% on Basic. This site did not confirm the individual fees of those external services this time. ColorMe Shop charges no sales commission, and the same payment methods sit inside the ColorMe Payment price table. That said, some of those methods also carry monthly fees or monthly minimums, and the choices narrow on the Free plan and for individual contracts. For a shop where cards account for most payments the difference is likely small; the more a product's customers pay by something other than a card, the more this difference is presumed to matter.
:::

## Working out a monthly cost (an illustration)

The following is an illustration calculated by this site using only the official numbers above. It is not an actual bill. The assumptions are:

- Every order is paid with a card issued in Japan (Visa/Mastercard).
- The Free plan charges 30 yen per transaction, so the calculation needs an order count; it assumes 5,000 yen per order. This is an assumption set by this site, not an official number.
- Initial fees, fees for apps and options, introductory discounts (such as Shopify's 150 yen for 3 months) and paid templates are not included.
- ColorMe Shop's prices are used as stated (tax included); Shopify's are used as stated (no tax statement found). The amounts are not on the same basis.
- For ColorMe Shop's "from" rates, the lower bound is used.

| Monthly sales | ColorMe Free | ColorMe Regular | Regular after Nov. revision | Shopify Basic, monthly | Shopify Basic, annual |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100,000 yen (20 orders) | 7,200 yen | 8,350 yen | 9,340 yen | 8,400 yen | 7,200 yen |
| 500,000 yen (100 orders) | 36,000 yen | 21,950 yen | 22,940 yen | 22,600 yen | 21,400 yen |

The arithmetic:

- Free: 100,000 × 6.6% + 30 × 20 = 6,600 + 600 = 7,200 yen. 500,000 × 6.6% + 30 × 100 = 33,000 + 3,000 = 36,000 yen. At 33 yen per transaction, these become 7,260 yen and 36,300 yen.
- Regular: 4,950 + 100,000 × 3.4% = 8,350 yen. 4,950 + 500,000 × 3.4% = 21,950 yen. After the revision, replace the monthly fee with 5,940 yen.
- Shopify Basic, paid monthly: 4,850 + 100,000 × 3.55% = 8,400 yen. 4,850 + 500,000 × 3.55% = 22,600 yen. For annual payment, replace the monthly fee with 3,650 yen.

:::guess
Within this illustration, at 100,000 yen in monthly sales every option lands between 7,200 and 9,340 yen, and at 500,000 yen only ColorMe Shop's Free plan pulls away. The gap between Regular and Shopify Basic ranges from 50 yen to 2,140 yen a month depending on the combination of terms, and given the differences in tax treatment and contract terms, it appears too small to decide on monthly fee and card rate alone. The difference is presumed to come from what the table leaves out: payment methods other than cards, monthly app fees, and the cost of templates.
:::

## Shipping, design, apps, selling abroad, support and invoices

### Shipping

:::fact
According to ColorMe Shop's feature pages, bulk registration of tracking numbers works with the shipping-label systems of major Japanese carriers such as Yamato Transport, Sagawa Express and Japan Post, and the data download feature exports order data as CSV in formats matched to each carrier's shipping labels. Both are listed as free on every plan including Free. In Shopify's Help Center, the fulfillment locations from which shipping labels can be bought in the admin are the United States, Canada, Australia, the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, Poland and Spain; when this site checked, Japan was not among them. The Shopify App Store carries an app called "Japan Order CSV" (Flagship Inc.) that exports CSV files for Yamato Transport, Sagawa Express, Japan Post and others; its listing shows a free plan and a plan at 29.99 US dollars a month. In the pricing table, real-time shipping rates from third-party carriers are listed as an Advanced feature.
:::

### Design

:::fact
According to ColorMe Shop's feature page, there are about 90 design templates, free and paid combined, and HTML/CSS editing is supported. The comparison table on the Free plan page also has a row for HTML/CSS editing. The paid templates in the template list were priced at 22,000 yen, 31,574 yen and 38,500 yen. Shopify's Theme Store listed 1,299 themes when checked on October 2, 2026. According to the Help Center, most theme files are written in Liquid, Shopify's templating language, and also contain HTML, CSS, JSON and JavaScript. The same page advises editing code only if you know HTML and CSS and have a basic understanding of Liquid, and warns that if your changes are incompatible with a theme update, they are removed in the updated copy.
:::

### Apps and extensions

:::fact
ColorMe Shop's official pages describe "more than 350" features and "more than 80" add-on features. Shopify's pricing page lists "integrates with thousands of apps" as a feature of every plan. For the API used to build custom apps, Basic is described as "limited access to data" and Grow and above as "full access to data."
:::

### Selling abroad

:::fact
ColorMe Shop's cross-border e-commerce page presents selling abroad as something you start "just by adding an app for your purpose," listing a proxy-selling service as a standard feature and apps such as WorldShopping BIZ, ZenLink, Buyee Connect Pro, Overseas Shipping Assist and shutto Translation as add-ons. The explanation is that you ship the product to a designated warehouse in Japan and leave packing, international shipping and customs handling to the proxy service. Shopify's pricing page lists storefront translation, localized selling with Shopify Markets, assigning local domains, and estimating and collecting duties and taxes in the table for every plan. It notes that showing prices in local currencies and supporting local payment methods require Shopify Payments.
:::

### Support in Japanese

:::fact
According to ColorMe Shop's pricing page, the target for email replies is within 2 business days on Regular, within 1 business day on Large, and within 1 business day from an EC advisor on Premium. Phone support is marked as available within Japan only. The Free plan page says support on the Free plan is the "ColorMe AI Chat" and email, with no phone guidance. Shopify's pricing page lists "24-hour support in Japanese" as available on every plan; in the table, Basic and Grow have live chat, Advanced has enhanced live chat, and Plus has priority support. According to the Help Center, chat is available around the clock on all plans, while phone and email support are limited to the Shopify Plus and Retail plans.
:::

### Invoices (qualified invoices)

Japan introduced a "qualified invoice" system in October 2023: a business customer can claim a consumption-tax credit only against an invoice that carries the seller's registration number and certain required items.

:::fact
According to ColorMe Shop's feature page, issuing and printing invoices, delivery slips and receipts is free on every plan (bulk printing is not available on the Free plan). The official feature list includes an app, "Invoice Kudasai for ColorMe Shop," described as letting the buyer generate documents including qualified invoices automatically, from 1,100 yen a month. Shopify's Help Center page on taxes in Japan explains that merchants in Japan are required to use tax-inclusive pricing. As far as this site checked, that page says nothing about issuing qualified invoices.
:::

Some things could not be confirmed. Whether ColorMe Shop's standard receipts and invoices satisfy the required items of a qualified invoice, such as the registration number, could not be read from the feature page. Whether Shopify's standard features can issue a qualified invoice was not stated on the official pages this site was able to check. A shop with many corporate customers, or customers who buy on expenses, would do well to ask both companies before signing up.

## Why they split this way

:::guess
The root of the difference appears to lie in what each service treats as its "core." ColorMe Shop is a domestic cart built on the premise of Japanese shops selling to Japanese customers, and it lines up konbini payment, carrier billing, deferred payment, shipping labels for domestic carriers and receipt printing on its price list as one package, together with a payment company in the same GMO group. Selling abroad, conversely, is placed on the side handled by apps and proxy services. Shopify is a platform on which shops around the world use the same core, and what the core holds is the part that does not depend on country: checkout, multiple languages, multiple currencies, duties. The parts that differ from country to country — in Japan, konbini payment and shipping labels — are opened up to external payment services and apps.
:::

:::guess
The way each charges is presumed to follow that structure too. As the [ColorMe Shop dissection](/en/articles/colorme-shop) showed, the pillar of its revenue is the monthly fee, and the sales commission is 0 yen; payment fees go to the payment processor. As the [Shopify dissection](/en/articles/shopify) showed, merchant solutions such as payment processing make up more than half of Shopify's revenue. Charging a third-party transaction fee on transactions that do not use its own payments, and lowering that rate on higher plans, reads as the shape of a platform that assumes transactions pass through its own payments. It is not a matter of one being good and the other bad; what a company earns from appears to decide the shape of its price list.
:::

:::pull
ColorMe Shop puts domestic payments and shipping in the core and leaves selling abroad to apps. Shopify puts selling abroad in the core and leaves domestic specifics to apps and external payment services. Before the monthly fee differs, the direction of the addition is reversed.
:::

## Which situation fits which

No ranking here. From the facts confirmed on the official pages, this sorts out which situations tend to match which design.

:::guess
The situations that appear to match ColorMe Shop's design are these:

- You do not know whether the shop will sell and want to start with zero fixed cost. The Free plan has no monthly fee and no initial fee; the only cost is the processing fee when something sells. Note that a shop cannot move back from a paid plan to the Free plan.
- Most of your customers are in Japan and your products call for payment methods other than cards, such as konbini payment or deferred payment.
- You want to ask questions in Japanese by phone. The paid plans include phone support.
- You can already write HTML and CSS and want to edit templates directly.

The situations that appear to match Shopify's design are these:

- You want to sell to customers abroad from the start. Translation, local currencies and duty estimates are in the table for every plan.
- Your products are ones where cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay cover most payments.
- You expect to add the functions you need through apps and want to choose from thousands of them. Monthly fees per app may apply, and they need to go into your estimate.
- You want a month-by-month contract. ColorMe Shop's paid plans start at three months.
:::

There is one more thing for an individual to check. ColorMe Payment has a separate table for individuals, and carrier billing cannot be used under an individual contract. On the Shopify side, this site did not confirm this time what conditions apply to the payment methods a sole proprietor can use.

## When switching comes up

:::guess
The moments likely to prompt a move away from ColorMe Shop are presumed to be when customers abroad increase and you want the core to handle multiple languages and currencies, when a function you want to add is not in the domestic app store, and when you review costs after the November 2026 revision raises the Regular and Large monthly fees. The moments likely to prompt a move away from Shopify appear to be when non-card payments grow and the third-party transaction fee gets heavier, when the total of monthly app fees swells, and when you want to issue shipping labels and documents without additional apps.
:::

:::guess
In either direction, switching appears to involve moving product data, customer data, design and URLs. ColorMe Shop's feature page says customer, product and sales data can be downloaded as CSV, but this site did not confirm how much work importing at the destination or rebuilding the design takes. Because the template systems are different, a design presumably cannot be carried over as is. The first choice can be changed later, but changing it takes work.
:::

On monthly fee alone, the two services are close. What differs is which of two things — what you need to sell in Japan, and what you need to sell abroad — went into the core and which became an add-on. Deciding first where your customers are and how they pay decides how to read the price list.
