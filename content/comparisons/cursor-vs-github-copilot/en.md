[Cursor](/en/articles/cursor) and [GitHub Copilot](/en/articles/github-copilot) are often compared on the same shelf, but in 2026 both rebuilt their price lists into new shapes. Copilot moved its billing unit from requests to tokens in June. Cursor began selling to new customers in October through the same subscription as Grok and X. This piece reads both companies' official pages on November 2, 2026 and lays out the numbers and conditions as written. We are not reporting on long-term hands-on use; we are comparing public price lists and documentation. Prices have moved a lot in recent months, so check the official pages before you subscribe.

## The pricing, side by side

:::fact
This table rearranges, by situation, what Cursor's pricing page and docs and Copilot's docs say (all accessed 2026-11-02). Prices are in US dollars on both; Cursor states that its prices exclude tax.

| Item | Cursor (The X Subscription) | GitHub Copilot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free | Free (limited Agent and Chat) | Free (2,000 completions a month; chat and agents within an auto-selection allowance) |
| Entry | Plus $8/month (own-pool models only, no Cloud Agents) | Pro $10/month (1,500 credits) |
| Mid | Premium $30/month (4x Plus) | Pro+ $39/month (7,000 credits) |
| Heavy | Super $100/month (15x Plus), Ultra $200/month (50x Plus) | Max $100/month (20,000 credits) |
| Teams | $30, $100 or $200 per seat/month | Business $19 per seat/month, Enterprise $39 per seat/month |
| Allowance unit | Weekly shared pool, shared with Grok usage | Monthly credits; unused credits do not roll over |
| What the allowance covers | Grok 4.7, 4.6, 4.5 and Composer 2.5 | Every model the plan offers, consumed at token rates |
| Other companies' models | Not in the allowance. API list price on Premium and up, from prepaid credit | Consumed within credits; set a budget for more |
| When it runs out | Wait, prepaid credit, or upgrade | Wait, extra budget, or upgrade for the difference |
| Business surcharge | $0.25 per million tokens on eligible third-party models (Cursor Token Rate) | None listed; credits are pooled across the organization |
| Completions | See Tab note below | Completions and next edit sit outside credits, unlimited on paid plans |

Cursor lists annual prices of $80 for Plus, $300 for Premium, $1,000 for Super and $2,000 for Ultra. Its legacy plans (Hobby, Pro at $20/month, Pro+ at $60/month, Ultra at $200/month, Teams Standard at $40/user/month, Teams Premium at $120/user/month) continue for existing customers, but new customers start on The X Subscription. Legacy Pro and similar plans are said to include unlimited Tab; in the X Subscription material we read we could not find an explicit statement on Tab.

Copilot kept its business plans with credits included in the price, and also offers Copilot access, with a seat charge of $0 or $10 and tokens billed from the first use. On tax, GitHub says that since October 2024 it collects 10% consumption tax from self-serve customers in Japan paying by card or PayPal.
:::

:::fact
Compare the same model and the list prices match. Here are the per-million-token US dollar rates from Cursor's Models & Pricing and Copilot's Models and pricing (accessed 2026-11-02).

| Model | Cursor input / cached / output | Copilot input / cached / output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Claude Opus 5.5 | 4 / 0.2 / 20 | 4 / 0.20 / 20 |
| Claude Fable 5.1 | 10 / 0.25 / 50 | 10 / 0.25 / 50 |
| Grok 4.7 (standard context) | 2 / 0.5 / 6 | 2 / 0.50 / 6 |
| Composer 2.5 | 0.5 / 0.2 / 2.5 | Not offered |
| MAI-Code-1.1-Flash | Not listed | 0.20 / 0.02 / 1.20 |

Cursor's own-pool rates apply beyond the weekly pool. Inside the pool, models are said to draw it down at different rates.
:::

:::guess
If the same API rates apply, the price difference appears not in token rates but in what the monthly fee brings. For someone centered on Claude, Copilot's $39 Pro+ comes with $70 of credits, while Cursor's $30 pool does not cover other companies' models, which are paid separately from prepaid credit. On that comparison alone, Copilot comes out ahead. If you mainly use Grok 4.7 or Composer 2.5, Cursor's weekly pool applies directly, and Cursor says the amount is at least twice the legacy plan's. Which is cheaper seems to depend almost entirely on your mix of models. Flex allotments are described as variable, so nothing guarantees how long this gap lasts.
:::

:::pull
When token rates match, the price gap moves from the tokens to whose models the monthly allowance covers.
:::

## Choosing models, and routing

:::fact
According to Cursor's docs, Auto has three modes, Cost, Balance and Intelligence, and individual plans have only Cost. Cost bills as a Cursor-model request from the weekly pool whichever model serves it. Cursor Router on Teams and Enterprise may also pick third-party models, in which case prepaid credit pays.

Copilot's Fusion picks among the Single, Cascade and Critique execution patterns per prompt and offers Standard, High and Max effort levels, with High and Max at Pro+ and up. You pay the standard rate for each model used, and paid plans get a 30% discount through November 30, 2026. Free and Student use auto selection only and cannot pick models. The model range varies by plan; Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-5.6 Sol start at Pro+.
:::

:::fact
On Cursor's side, the relationship with OpenAI is in motion. According to CNBC (August 29, 2026), OpenAI announced it would end access to its models through Cursor after the acquisition and proposed November 12, 2026 as the shutoff date. Cursor's CEO said OpenAI models serve about 5% of user traffic and that the companies were talking. As of November 2, 2026, Cursor's docs still list OpenAI models, including the GPT-5.6 family, with many older models hidden by default. We could not confirm what will happen after the proposed date.

Copilot's plan comparison page lists models from Anthropic, OpenAI, Microsoft, Google, xAI and Moonshot. How each provider's models are hosted and treated for training is set out by provider on the official hosting page.
:::

## Agents and review

:::fact
Both have agents that run tasks in the cloud and return pull requests.

| Item | Cursor Cloud Agents | Copilot cloud agent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plans | Premium and up, Teams, Enterprise, legacy paid plans | Pro and up (Business and Enterprise subject to policy) |
| Runtime | Isolated VMs, configured with snapshots or a Dockerfile | GitHub Actions or cloud sandboxes |
| Where code can live | GitHub, GitLab, Azure DevOps Services, Bitbucket Cloud | GitHub only |
| Multiple repositories | Can open PRs in each repo it changes | Sandboxes support multi-repository sessions in one organization; one PR per task |
| Time limit | None stated in what we read | 59 minutes on Actions, not extendable |
| Cost | Draws on the plan's usage | AI credits, plus Actions or sandbox meters |
| Other companies' agents | Not listed | Claude and Codex in public preview |

For code review, Cursor's Bugbot is included from Premium, and Copilot's is available from Pro. Copilot's review is estimated at about $1.50 on average per review at the default Balanced level, with Actions minutes on top.

Cursor also offers its own git forge, Origin, in early beta. It is said to let you create repositories, mirror from GitHub, and open, review and merge pull requests. Copilot, conversely, states it does not work with repositories hosted outside GitHub.
:::

:::guess
What really matters when comparing agents seems to be where your code lives. For GitLab or Bitbucket users, Copilot's cloud agent is not an option, so Cursor comes first. Organizations on GitHub likely benefit from reusing Copilot's permissions, audit and Actions settings as they are. Handling other companies' agents on one screen also means something to teams whose model preferences differ. Cursor's Origin can be read as an attempt to pull the agent's workplace itself in-house, but for now it is an early beta.
:::

## Data and training

:::fact
Treatment of training data can be sorted by default setting and business plan.

| Item | Cursor | GitHub Copilot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Individual setting | With Privacy Mode on, Cursor does not train on customer data. With it off, codebase data, prompts and more may be stored and used for training | Since April 24, 2026, interaction data on Free, Pro, Pro+ and Max may be used for training; can be set to Disabled |
| Business default | On by default for all Teams members and for Enterprise; admins can enforce it | Business and Enterprise customer data is not used for training |
| Third parties | ZDR agreements with all providers under Privacy Mode | Agreements per provider; training data is not shared with third-party model providers |
| Exceptions | Non-ZDR models are labeled or need admin approval | Claude Fable models are not covered by ZDR by default. Student, free-teacher and paid-organization accounts are excluded from training |

Cursor's help does not state the default of Privacy Mode for individuals. It also explains that Privacy Mode does not extend to separate products such as Grok Bot.
:::

:::guess
Both products clearly promise no training for businesses and ask individuals to check a setting. Anyone handling work code on a personal account would be safest confirming Privacy Mode on Cursor, or the training setting on Copilot, right at sign-up. Moving to The X Subscription presumably also brings the Grok products into what you need to check.
:::

## Which fits which kind of developer

What follows is our reading of the tables above. It does not rank the products; for each way of working, it shows which rows matter.

:::guess
- Individuals keeping code on GitHub and mainly using Claude or GPT models will probably find Copilot's price list a better match. Pro+ is $39 a month with $70 of credits, and Opus 5.5 draws on them. It also works in Visual Studio, JetBrains IDEs, Eclipse and Xcode.
- People who mostly use completions and only occasionally use agents may find Copilot Pro at $10 a month a reasonable fit, since completions consume no credits.
- People centered on Grok 4.7 or Composer 2.5 who want agents inside the editor are likely to get more from Cursor's pool. Those who already use X benefits or Grok products also gain from consolidating. Plus excludes Cloud Agents, though, so Premium or above is needed.
- Teams keeping code on GitLab, Bitbucket or Azure DevOps may find Cursor Cloud Agents' coverage decisive.
- Organizations already on GitHub Enterprise are likely to value running Copilot's seats, pooled credits, policies and audit logs together. On seat price alone, Business at $19 a month is below Cursor's minimum Teams seat at $30. The allowances differ, though, so seat price alone cannot settle it.
- Cursor users whose work depends on OpenAI models should probably check what happens around the proposed November 12, 2026 date before deciding.
- Existing Cursor legacy-plan users should note that switching is optional and described as permanent.
:::

## Why the price lists are shaped differently

:::fact
As our [Cursor dissection](/en/articles/cursor) explains, Cursor started as an editor built on the VS Code codebase, and its blog index shows it announced joining SpaceX on August 14, 2026. Its pricing page now names X Subscription Corp as the seller. As our [Copilot dissection](/en/articles/github-copilot) explains, Copilot began as a completion feature of GitHub, the company Microsoft announced it would buy in 2018. GitHub's April post called the premium request model no longer sustainable, and Microsoft's earnings call (July 29, 2026) said margins improved through the quarter after the June change.
:::

:::guess
The shapes of the price lists seem to come from what each company owns. Cursor's parent has GPUs and Grok, so the price centers on an own-model pool, with other models outside it at API prices. The more users move to own models, the more cost stays inside the group. A weekly pool can also be read as a design that smooths use of compute.

GitHub's parent has Azure and a non-exclusive range of models, and GitHub's largest asset is not a model but the place where repositories and pull requests live. So Copilot's price is built to buy every model with one credit, Fusion and a light first-party model bring cost down, and other companies' agents are invited into its house.

Both are, of course, stepping onto the other's ground. Cursor reaches into git hosting with Origin; Copilot reaches into local work with the Copilot app and CLI. Even so, each price list reflects what its parent wants to earn from.
:::

Cursor and Copilot sell the same models at the same rates while bundling different things into the monthly fee. Cursor bundles its own models, the experience inside its editor and the wider X subscription. Copilot bundles credits at API rates with the flow of work inside GitHub. What a buyer should look at is less whose benchmark is higher than which models you mainly use, where your code lives, and whose data terms you work under. Settle those three, and it becomes clear which rows of the two price lists to read.
