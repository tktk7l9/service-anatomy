[Studio](/en/articles/studio) and [Webflow](/en/articles/webflow) are both presented as tools that take you from design to hosting without writing code. Put their pricing and official explanations side by side, though, and they differ in who they imagine using them and in how much they hold for you. This article reads both companies' official pages on November 2, 2026 and sets out the numbers and conditions they state. We are comparing public information, not reporting on running a site on either service. Pricing and limits change, so always check the official pages before signing up. Webflow's help center refused our requests, so what we say about Webflow stays within its pricing and product pages.

## The pricing, side by side

:::fact
The table below rearranges what Studio's pricing page and help center and Webflow's pricing page say (all viewed 2026-11-02). Studio's terms state that displayed prices include tax. Webflow's pricing page says "All prices in USD, per site, plus applicable taxes added at checkout". Webflow's monthly figures are as shown for yearly billing, and the page says yearly billing saves up to 40%.

| Item | Studio | Webflow |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free plan | Free (50 pages, 3 CMS models, 100 published items, 5GB storage, 100 viewable form responses) | Starter (webflow.io domain, 2 static pages, 1GB bandwidth, 50 form submissions, 50 CMS items) |
| Cheapest paid plan | Mini, ¥590 a month billed yearly, ¥1,290 monthly (2 pages + 404 page, custom domain) | Basic, $15 a month billed yearly (300 static pages, 10GB bandwidth, custom domain, no CMS) |
| Personal CMS | Personal, ¥1,190 a month billed yearly, ¥1,720 monthly (150 pages, 5 CMS models, 1,000 published items) | No equivalent; CMS starts at Premium |
| Business CMS | Business, ¥3,980 a month billed yearly, ¥5,460 monthly (300 pages, 10 models, 5,000 published items) | Premium, from $25 a month billed yearly (20,000 CMS items, 40 Collections, bandwidth from 50GB to 2.5TB) |
| Higher tier | Business Plus, ¥9,980 a month billed yearly, ¥12,900 monthly (30 models, 15,000 published items, 1,000 concurrent connections guaranteed) | Team, $2,500 a month (annual contract required; includes Localize, AEO agents and more) |
| Top tier | Enterprise (contact sales) | Enterprise (contact sales) |
| Published-site SLA | 99.9%, Enterprise only | Hosting uptime in the table: 99.00% for Team, 99.99% for Enterprise |
| Traffic limits | Monthly visitor limits removed from all plans in July 2026 | Bandwidth limits per plan |
| Team management | Team workspace (¥5,940 monthly, 3 seats included) | Separate line of Workspace plans |
| Payment | Cards with 3-D Secure (JPY if country of residence is Japan); Enterprise by prepaid bank transfer | USD, as on the pricing page |
| Cancelling mid-term | In principle no refund | You can cancel any time but pay the rest of the term |
:::

:::fact
Studio is registered as a qualified invoice issuer under Japan's invoice system, and its help center says invoices are issued for paid plans. We could not confirm anything on Japanese qualified invoices within Webflow's pricing page.
:::

:::guess
Looking at the units of billing is likely to help more than converting currencies and ranking the result. Studio's limits are pages and CMS counts, and it has a very small plan in Mini. Webflow's are static pages plus bandwidth, and rising traffic moves you up Premium's options. Having removed traffic limits in 2026, Studio has less risk of a sudden spike changing what you pay. On the other hand, Studio's Business and Business Plus share the same 300-page limit. A dynamic CMS page counts as one page, so a site with thousands of articles is unaffected, but a site with more than 300 fixed pages would apparently need an Enterprise conversation.
:::

## How you build

:::fact
Studio's official pages compare the work to building with blocks: layouts are combinations of boxes, and responsive behavior is handled automatically. A plugin converts Figma designs by pasting. The editor page says 9,200+ fonts are free to use and names Morisawa's TypeSquare. Webflow's Design page says you have complete control over layout, from flexbox and CSS grid to inline block and absolute positioning. According to its pricing page, Premium includes code components, so developer-written or AI-generated components can be placed on the canvas.
:::

:::guess
The order of learning seems to differ. With Studio, learning boxes and responsive settings gets you a result, and it looks easier to proceed without CSS knowledge. Webflow's first hill is steeper, but what you learn is CSS itself, so it stays with you as standard knowledge. If in-house marketers or non-engineering designers will update the site, Studio's lower entry matters. If someone with frontend knowledge wants fine control, Webflow's vocabulary matters. If you care how Japanese body text looks, Studio's official statement that Morisawa typefaces are free is a strength. We did not check how licensing would work if you tried to do the same on Webflow.
:::

## Export and ways out

This is the largest single difference between the two.

:::fact
Studio's help center says design data, page data, the HTML, CSS and JavaScript of the published site, and CMS data cannot be exported, and that sites can be published only on Studio's servers. Only form responses can be downloaded, as CSV. Webflow's pricing FAQ says that on any paid Workspace plan you can export your site and host it anywhere, with no attribution required. It adds a note: dynamic content must be exported collection by collection, and forms will stop working. It also says exported code cannot be reimported and that websites can be developed only in Webflow.
:::

:::fact
Connections to the outside differ as well. Studio's Data Connect API, on Business and above, shows external data from Notion, microCMS and others in dynamic lists and pages. Business allows one list endpoint, and Business Plus and above have no limit. Studio offers no cart or payment feature, so that means embedding or linking to outside services. Webflow's pricing page lists Webflow Cloud, with overage charged by requests and CPU time, and its product page says Next.js and Astro apps run on Cloudflare Workers. It also sells separate Ecommerce site plans.
:::

:::pull
Studio builds no way out; Webflow leaves a narrow one. The gap starts to matter when you imagine a site lasting ten years.
:::

:::guess
Webflow's export does not look like a complete moving service either. As its own note says, forms stop working and CMS content leaves one collection at a time. Even so, having HTML and CSS in hand is very different from starting over. If a client's internal rules demand an exit in case a vendor shuts down, Webflow is probably easier to justify than Studio. For an organization with no developers, which could not use exported code anyway, the gap matters less. If you want app features or payments in the same place, Webflow's range is wider.
:::

## How AI comes in

:::fact
Studio's editor includes Image AI, Text AI, Auto Responsive, Layer Rename, SEO Writing Assist and Embed Code AI, with usage limits. Studio.Assistant, sold separately from ¥19,800 a month billed yearly, covers everything from hearing sheets to drafts. Studio.Drop is a new service that takes in a live site and runs it through conversation with AI. It advertises MCP connections to Claude and Codex and was an invite-only closed beta as of 2026-11-02. Webflow's pricing page lists Webflow AI and an MCP server even on the free Starter, and its product page describes building sites, generating copy and code, and improving SEO and AEO. The Team plan includes 100,000 shared AI credits a year. Source by Webflow, a new platform for marketing teams and agents, is in a limited research preview.
:::

:::guess
Both companies appear to point toward handing operations to agents. The difference is in how they sell it. Studio charges a relatively high separate fee for Assistant, which compresses the early stages of production, while Webflow builds AI into the platform on credits and puts AEO agents in its upper plans for companies. Since the new products on both sides have only just begun, choosing on today's editor experience seems safer than choosing on this.
:::

## Security and what you hand over

:::fact
Studio's security page describes a Google Cloud setup across Tokyo and Iowa regions, a Google Cloud Armor WAF and enforced TLS 1.3 or later, and its pricing page lists ISO/IEC 27001:2022 and ISO/IEC 27017:2015. SAML 2.0 and OIDC SSO come with the Enterprise plan. Webflow's security page lists ISO 27001, ISO 27017, ISO 27018, ISO 42001 and SOC 2, and says it maintains SOC 2 Type II compliance. It offers TLS 1.2 or later on all sites and encryption of data at rest, and has a vulnerability disclosure program.
:::

:::fact
On October 16, 2026, Studio disclosed that a third party had gained unauthorized access to its cloud environment and that, for all users, account data, design and CMS content, form submissions, API keys for outside services and more may have been exposed. It says it has not confirmed that anything was actually taken but is treating it as taken. The vulnerability has been fixed; as of our reading on 2026-11-02, the counts, the secondary-damage findings, and the cause and prevention measures were still to come. We did not research past security incidents at Webflow for this comparison, so this is not a like-for-like judgment of the two.
:::

:::guess
Whichever you choose, collecting personal data through forms means the responses are stored in the service provider's environment. In Studio's case, responses sent while saving was disabled and external embeds such as Google Forms were said to be out of scope. For a site that handles a lot of personal data, it may be worth considering not storing responses, or using a dedicated form service, on either platform. Existing Studio users should confirm they have changed passwords and API keys as the official guidance asks. Anyone choosing now would do well to include the cause and prevention measures, once published, in their decision.
:::

## Which fits whom

From here on is our own reading of the conditions above. It is not a ranking, only an account of which conditions matter.

:::guess
- If you want a small Japanese site or landing page on your own domain cheaply, Studio is likely the better fit. Mini is ¥590 a month billed yearly, the smallest start available if two pages are enough.
- For corporate or recruiting sites updated by in-house marketers or non-engineers, Studio's low entry, yen billing and chat support are likely to matter. Business, at ¥3,980 a month billed yearly, gives you 300 pages and 10 CMS models.
- If you have frontend knowledge and want control down to CSS details, or a team running multilanguage sites for overseas audiences, Webflow looks like the better fit. Team includes Localize with two locales.
- For projects that need an exit in case a vendor shuts down, Webflow's export is a deciding factor. Studio has no exit.
- For media sites beyond 10,000 articles, Studio's Business Plus has a guideline of 15,000 items in total and 10,000 per model, and Webflow's Premium has 20,000. Check both limits and estimate early whether your future scale means Enterprise.
- If you want apps or payments running beside the site, Webflow's range with Webflow Cloud and Ecommerce is wider. On Studio you combine outside services.
- Agencies and freelancers delivering to Japanese clients should weigh how delivered sites accumulate as Studio subscriptions, how Studio's Store and Works function, and what they will have to explain to clients. On Webflow, the gap between individual site plans and Team at $2,500 a month is wide.
:::

## Why the designs differ so much

:::fact
According to our [Studio article](/en/articles/studio), Studio is a Japanese company founded in 2016 with 900,000+ users and 250,000+ published sites, and the three rounds in its history total ¥530 million. Webflow's official site gives 2013 as its founding year, 300,000 organizations using it, and $335M in funding to date.
:::

:::guess
The difference in design appears to come from which market each looked at first. Webflow seems to have earned the trust of professionals in the English-speaking web world by making CSS, their common language, into the tool itself and leaving export as a way out. Studio seems to have gone the other way for Japanese designers and small businesses, removing servers and code from view altogether.

As a result, Webflow has differentiated by staying close to web standards and Studio by taking on everything. Each road has its price. Webflow's is the weight of learning, and Studio's is the breadth of what you hand over.
:::

Rather than asking which is better, it gets you closer to an answer to ask who will update your site, how many years it will run, and whether it will ever need to leave. If anyone in the company should be able to touch it, the Japanese should look good and you want to forget about servers, Studio's conditions fit. If someone who knows CSS will design it, it will go abroad and you want an exit secured, Webflow's conditions fit. The difference in the price tables can wait until after that.
