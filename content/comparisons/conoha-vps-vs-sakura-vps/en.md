[ConoHa VPS](/en/articles/conoha-vps) and [Sakura VPS](/en/articles/sakura-vps) both rent out virtual servers with root access in Japan. Laying the two anatomy articles side by side, the differences show less in the server parts than in what the price is measured by, whether you choose a location, and how the promise for downtime is written.

This page was put together from both services' official pages as read on November 2, 2026. This site has not rented both under the same conditions to measure speed or stability, so it does not judge which feels better. All prices include tax.

## Price tags: ConoHa cuts by time and term, Sakura by location and billing cycle

:::fact
According to ConoHa VPS's pricing and specifications page, hourly billing never exceeds a monthly cap, and Matome-Toku prepays a term of 1, 3, 6, 12, 24 or 36 months in one payment. That day's price list shows the same Matome-Toku amounts for first contract and renewal. Sakura VPS's pricing and specifications page shows prices by switching between the Ishikari, Osaka and Tokyo regions, with payment either monthly or every 12 months and no initial fee. For data transfer, ConoHa says there is no charge and Sakura says it is unlimited.
:::

| Memory (monthly, tax included) | ConoHa hourly cap | ConoHa 1 month | ConoHa 12 months | ConoHa 36 months | Sakura Ishikari monthly | Sakura Ishikari 12-month equivalent | Sakura Tokyo monthly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 GB | 1,065 yen | 763 yen | 588 yen | 542 yen | 880 yen | 807 yen | 990 yen |
| 2 GB | 2,033 yen | 1,259 yen | 980 yen | 903 yen | 1,738 yen | 1,594 yen | 1,958 yen |
| 4 GB | 3,608 yen | 2,189 yen | 1,791 yen | 1,691 yen | 3,520 yen | 3,227 yen | 3,960 yen |

:::fact
Virtual CPUs match: 2 cores at 1 GB, 3 at 2 GB and 4 at 4 GB. For disks, ConoHa gives 100 GB on all three, while Sakura gives 50 GB at 1 GB, 100 GB at 2 GB and 200 GB at 4 GB.
:::

Comparing the same 2 GB and 100 GB combination on a 12-month term, ConoHa's 980 yen a month is 614 yen below Sakura Ishikari's monthly equivalent of 1,594 yen (this site's calculation). At 4 GB the gap widens to 1,436 yen, but Sakura has twice the disk, so price alone does not settle it.

:::guess
ConoHa appears to fit its price tag to the length of use, taking short stays on hourly billing and discounting by term for longer ones. Sakura can be read as keeping to two billing cycles and making location the axis of the price tag instead. This comparison uses the November 2, 2026 tables, though, and since ConoHa sometimes lowers first-contract prices during campaigns, the gap presumably changes with the day you compare.
:::

## Location and stock: Sakura's regions and a stock notice

:::fact
Sakura VPS's pricing and specifications page shows Tokyo above Ishikari for the same plan. Billed monthly, 2 GB costs 1,738 yen in Ishikari, 1,848 yen in Osaka and 1,958 yen in Tokyo. An October 2, 2026 notice says stock of the 4 GB, 8 GB, 16 GB and 32 GB Linux plans in the Ishikari region is tight and that new orders or plan changes may not be possible depending on the situation. An addendum at the top dated October 15 says the 32 GB shortage was resolved; replenishment is still described as "within this month," and when checked on November 2, 2026 no notice announcing its completion could be found. The notice says the Tokyo and Osaka regions can be ordered.
:::

:::guess
With Sakura, choosing the cheapest region and finding stock there appear to be separate questions. Anyone planning around the Ishikari price would likely want to check stock on the day of ordering and see whether the budget also works at Tokyo or Osaka prices.
:::

## The promise for downtime: what the SLAs cover and how they compensate

:::fact
ConoHa's SLA page sets a monthly server uptime of 99.99% or more and, below that, grants service credit equal to 10% of the monthly fee between 99.9% and 99.99% and 30% below 99.9%. Sakura VPS's quality assurance page sets 99.95% monthly uptime for Linux plans with 4 GB of memory or more on V5 or later, reduces the fee by the portion that fell short, and excludes Sakura VPS for Windows Server. The pricing page likewise marks the SLA as "非対応" (not supported) from 512 MB to 2 GB and "対応" (supported) at 4 GB.
:::

| | ConoHa VPS | Sakura VPS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly uptime promised | 99.99% | 99.95% |
| Coverage | The SLA page says it covers ConoHa as a whole | Linux plans of 4 GB or more on V5 or later |
| If it falls short | Service credit of 10% or 30% of the monthly fee | Fee reduced by the portion that fell short |

:::guess
ConoHa's compensation is a set percentage per band, while Sakura's is worded in proportion to the shortfall. Beyond the height of the number, the difference between the two promises presumably shows when you read whether your plan is covered and how much comes back after a short outage.
:::

## Starting and stopping: trials, minimum terms and cancelling mid-term

:::fact
According to Sakura VPS's sign-up flow page, paying by credit card gives every plan a two-week free trial (up to two servers at once), which turns into a full registration automatically when it ends, and the minimum term is three months. ConoHa VPS's pricing page notes that Matome-Toku cannot be cancelled mid-term or paid in monthly installments, and that servers are deleted at the end of the term if the contract is not renewed.
:::

The questions for the buyer come down to how long, where, and how much memory. For someone who wants to try a server for a few days or weeks, or rent 1 GB or 2 GB cheaply for a long time, ConoHa VPS, with hourly billing and long prepaid terms, is a candidate. For someone who wants to start with a two-week free trial, choose among Ishikari, Osaka and Tokyo, or get a larger disk at 4 GB, Sakura VPS is a candidate. Either way, it is worth checking the price list and any stock notices on the day of signing up.
