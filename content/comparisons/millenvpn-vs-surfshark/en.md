[MillenVPN](/en/articles/millenvpn) and [Surfshark](/en/articles/surfshark) both sell, on Japanese-language sites, a VPN that encrypts traffic and hides the user's IP address, as a prepaid long-term subscription. Laying the two anatomy articles on top of each other, the difference appears to lie less in how deep the discounts go than in where each writes its renewal price, how each explains what it logs and for how long, and how each rewards referrers.

This article was put together from both services' official pages read on October 13, 2026. This site did not subscribe to either service to compare speed or ease of connection, and does not rank them on that. MillenVPN's pricing page gives prices both before and with tax, and Surfshark's pricing page shows tax-exclusive amounts with the note "（付加価値税が適用されます）" (VAT applies). So that the figures can be compared on the same footing, the tables in this article show amounts before tax (MillenVPN's tax-inclusive amounts in parentheses).

## Company and jurisdiction: a notified telecom carrier in Osaka and a B.V. in Amsterdam

:::fact
According to AzPocket, Inc.'s company profile (as of 2026-10-13), the company was founded on April 11, 2016, has its head office in Minamisenba, Chuo-ku, Osaka, and holds notified telecommunications carrier number E-28-03926. MillenVPN's features page describes MillenVPN as a service that AzPocket, which runs a telecommunications business in Japan, operates after notifying the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications, and says it is run in compliance with the secrecy of communications set out in Article 21 of the Constitution of Japan. The top page promotes it as "日本の法令に基づくクリーンなVPN" (a clean VPN based on Japanese law). According to Surfshark's media center (as of 2026-10-13), Surfshark VPN is provided by Surfshark B.V., a company registered in the Netherlands, which was originally founded in Lithuania in 2018. Surfshark's terms of service (updated July 13, 2026) apply the laws of the British Virgin Islands to the contract and ask users to agree that disputes are brought before the courts of the British Virgin Islands unless mandatory provisions of the law of their country of residence apply.
:::

| Operator | AzPocket, Inc. (Osaka, founded 2016) | Surfshark B.V. (registered in the Netherlands, founded in Lithuania in 2018) |

Both discount the first term deeply and sell long prepaid terms. Where they differ is where they write the renewal price and how they explain what they log.

## Pricing: a renewal price on the same page, and a renewal price on another page

:::fact
According to MillenVPN's pricing page (checked 2026-10-13), the first term of the 2-year plan costs 360 yen a month (396 yen with tax) and the first term of the 1-year plan 540 yen a month (594 yen with tax), and both show "更新時 ¥950 +税 /月" (on renewal: ¥950 + tax a month) in the same box. The monthly figures are per-month amounts when the whole term is paid at once; contracts renew automatically and the discount applies to the first term only. There are also one-time plans for 7, 15 and 30 days (638, 1,078 and 1,738 yen with tax), and the 30-day money-back guarantee applies to subscription plans only. Surfshark's pricing page (checked 2026-10-13) shows Starter's 24-month plan at 388 yen a month with a "+ 3 延長月" (+3 extra months) label and writes, against a struck-through 68,796 yen, that "初めの 27ヶ月間は" (for the first 27 months) the "セール価格 ￥10,476 です （付加価値税が適用されます）" (sale price is ¥10,476, VAT applies). The billing practices page says all subscription plans "renew automatically at the regular price" and that users are notified by email 30 days before the end date (except for 1-month plans). The renewal price table on the same page, however, was empty in the HTML this site fetched and is drawn by a script. In the data embedded in the pricing page's HTML, as read by this site, Starter's 12-month plan costs 7,920 yen for 15 months (3 extra months included), its 1-month plan 2,548 yen, and its renewal field (recurringTotalPrice) 12,508 yen every 12 months (all before tax). The pricing page's FAQ says any number of devices can be connected at the same time, and every plan carries a 30-day money-back guarantee. The terms of service apply the guarantee only to the first purchase, once per service.
:::

The first-term monthly price is 360 yen on MillenVPN's 2-year plan and 388 yen on Surfshark's 24-month plan; both appear to lower the entry price with a deep discount on a long prepaid term and recoup it at renewal. The difference shows in how the renewal price is presented. MillenVPN puts the first-term and renewal prices in the same box, so users can read the monthly price two years out before they sign up. Surfshark keeps the renewal amount off the pricing page and places it in a table on another page that is drawn according to the visitor's location, under a caveat about A/B price testing. This reads either as a design suited to selling with prices swapped by country and currency, or as a design that puts the low first-term price up front.
:::

## What is logged: separate rules per connection method, and deletion within 15 minutes

:::fact
MillenVPN's privacy policy (effective 2026-08-25, checked 2026-10-13) is written separately for MillenVPN (the app), MillenVPN Native, dedicated servers and the fixed IP option. For the app, it says it neither collects nor records source IP addresses and timestamps, communication content, browsing history or DNS queries; for Native, it does not record communication content and the like but does collect source IP addresses and timestamps (without communication content). For dedicated servers and the fixed IP option, it collects the assigned fixed IP address and the period of assignment, and states that the subscriber can be identified from an IP address and time of use reported in an outside inquiry. Surfshark's privacy policy (updated October 12, 2026, checked 2026-10-13) says it does not collect information about online activity such as the IP addresses of sites visited or browsing history, and then says that for connections to a particular VPN server it temporarily stores the user ID, IP address, connection timestamp and similar details and deletes them automatically within 15 minutes after the session ends. Surfshark's official blog of January 25, 2023 announced that Deloitte had performed independent assurance procedures on its no-logs policy, and its official blog of June 16, 2025 announced a second assurance by Deloitte. The MillenVPN official pages this site read contained no statement of results from a third-party audit or assurance.
:::

| Item | MillenVPN | Surfshark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source IP address and timestamp | Not collected for the app and the fixed IP option; collected for Native and dedicated servers | User ID, IP address and connection timestamp stored temporarily and deleted within 15 minutes after the session ends |
| Communication content and browsing history | Neither collected nor recorded for any method | Not collected |
| Identifying the subscriber | Stated as possible from an IP address and time of use for the fixed IP option and dedicated servers | — |
| Third-party check | Not stated on official pages (within what this site read) | Assurance by Deloitte (2023 and 2025, per the official blog) |

:::guess
MillenVPN writes the logging scope separately for each connection method and states in its contract language that the subscriber can be identified with a fixed IP. This appears to be a line drawn so that a consumer VPN and a fixed IP, whose value lies precisely in knowing whose traffic it is, can be sold under the same brand. Surfshark appears to back up trust by stating that it keeps connection data only briefly and by showing that an accounting firm has given assurance on its compliance with that description. However, the body of Surfshark's assurance report is available only to account holders, and this site has not read it. Both "no-logs" claims appear best taken as each company's own description.
:::

## Devices, servers and fixed IPs: how each describes its Japanese locations

:::fact
According to MillenVPN's service comparison page (checked 2026-10-13), the app uses WireGuard, IKEv2 and OpenVPN, MillenVPN Native uses IKEv2 and MillenVPN Native OpenConnect uses OpenConnect; the Japanese servers for all of them are in Tokyo and Osaka, and the app can use more than 2,000 servers in 140 locations. The fair use policy says the limit on simultaneous connections was removed on November 25, 2024, and gives 3 TB a month, for each of the app, Native and OpenConnect, as the guideline data transfer at which resources may be limited. According to the pricing page, the fixed IP option costs from 360 yen a month (396 yen with tax) per IP (the per-month amount when the term is paid at once), requires a subscription plan, and is available in Tokyo, London, Paris, Frankfurt and Kansas City. Surfshark's protocols page (checked 2026-10-13) lists Dausos, WireGuard, IKEv2 and OpenVPN, and its page on servers in Japan says there are 80 servers in Tokyo and more than 4,500 worldwide. The Japanese version of the dedicated IP page lists 18 locations worldwide, and Japan is not among them.
:::

:::guess
Removing device limits and managing heavy use through fair-use rules appears to point the same way at both companies. For use from Japan, MillenVPN sells itself on having locations in both Tokyo and Osaka and on including Tokyo among its fixed IP regions, while Surfshark has servers in Tokyo but does not list Japan on the Japanese version of its dedicated IP page. For someone who needs a fixed Japanese IP, the conditions of MillenVPN's fixed IP option read as easier to confirm.
:::

## Referrals: a fixed fee through A8.net, and 40% of new sales

:::fact
According to MillenVPN's affiliate page (checked 2026-10-13), MillenVPN advertises through the ASP A8.net and pays a reward of up to 3,500 yen when a contract is concluded. Surfshark's affiliate page (checked 2026-10-13) lists a 40% revenue share on new sales, a 30-day cookie and a minimum payout of 100 dollars, and names Tunes (HasOffers) as its main platform. Surfshark's affiliate terms ban bidding on keywords identical or similar to Surfshark's marks and also ban posting refunds, credits or discounts without prior written permission.
:::

:::guess
MillenVPN uses a Japanese ASP as its window and states a per-sale cap, while Surfshark states a rate proportional to sales and tightly governs how affiliates may advertise through its own terms. From a referrer's point of view, MillenVPN appears to be a fixed-fee model that is easy to start even for a small Japanese site, and Surfshark a revenue-linked model whose terms are easier to negotiate the more volume one brings. Because Surfshark's terms require permission to post discounts, a comparison article that mentions sale prices would presumably need to check before carrying a Surfshark referral link.
:::

## Six shared tech stack items

The tech stack comparison shown below this page is a mechanical match of the two articles' techStack entries. The items judged as shared are Cloudflare, IKEv2, OpenVPN, Stripe, WireGuard and WordPress, six in all; how each is used is covered in the tech stack section of each article.

The question for the buyer can largely be framed as whether you want a Japanese company and yen prices, with the post-renewal price known before you sign, or a service that bundles scam protection and antivirus with the VPN and starts with a deep first-term discount. If you need a fixed Japanese IP or a short one-time plan, MillenVPN is a candidate; if you weigh the number of overseas locations or the bundled features more heavily, Surfshark is. Whichever you choose, it is worth checking the renewal price and the logging scope on the official pages before signing up.
