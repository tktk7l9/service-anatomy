[Money Forward Cloud](/en/articles/moneyforward-cloud) and [Yayoi](/en/articles/yayoi) are often weighed side by side when a sole proprietor or freelancer in Japan picks tax-return software. This piece checks both companies' official pages on October 1, 2026, sorts out the differences in price and function, and then reads why the two price lists are built differently. This site has not subscribed to or tested either product; everything here rests on published official information. Prices and campaigns change, so check the official pages before signing up.

Three Japan-specific terms appear throughout. A "blue return" (aoiro shinkoku) is a filing status for the self-employed that requires proper bookkeeping and grants a special deduction in return; a "white return" (shiroiro shinkoku) is the simpler default without it. "e-Tax" is the National Tax Agency's online filing system. The "invoice system" is the qualified-invoice regime for consumption tax (Japan's VAT); a business that registers as an invoice issuer becomes a taxable business and has to file a consumption-tax return.

## Comparing prices — first year and year two onward

:::fact
According to the Money Forward Cloud Tax Return pricing page, there are three plans for individuals, and all listed prices exclude tax. According to the company's notice, base fees for paid sole-proprietor contracts are revised on December 1, 2026; for contracts already in force on that date, the revised fees apply from the renewal that falls on or after December 1.

| Plan | Annual billing (now) | Annual billing (from December 1, 2026) | Monthly billing (now → revised) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Personal Mini | ¥10,800/year | ¥10,800/year (unchanged) | ¥1,280 → ¥1,680 |
| Personal | ¥15,360/year | ¥22,560/year | ¥1,680 → ¥2,880 |
| Personal Plus | ¥35,760/year | ¥38,160/year | Annual billing only |

There is a one-month free trial, and the pricing page says there is no automatic move to a paid plan. It also says the trial needs no credit card. Paid plans can be paid by credit card only; bank debit and invoice payment are not available. The notice also lists a monthly-only "Personal Mini App Plan" (¥1,500 → ¥1,880, tax included).
:::

:::fact
According to Yayoi's pricing page, Yayoi Blue Return Online has three plans, with prices written as "+ tax" (tax-exclusive). Features are identical across the three; the only difference is the scope of support.

| Plan | First year | From the second year |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Self | Free | ¥11,800/year + tax |
| Basic | Free | ¥22,800/year + tax |
| Total | ¥19,800/year + tax | ¥39,600/year + tax |

The first-year prices come from a "first year free" campaign that covers applications made by March 15, 2027 and applies once per business. The pricing page also says the campaign may change or end without notice. Using it requires registering bank debit or a credit card as the payment method for automatic renewal. A notice email arrives before renewal, and the contract can be cancelled until the last day of the month before the month in which it ends. Separately, there is a free trial plan of up to two months that excludes the closing and filing functions.
:::

:::fact
According to the Yayoi White Return Online pricing page, the Free plan offers "all functions free indefinitely," with web FAQ as the only support. The Basic plan is free for the first year and ¥11,500/year + tax after the free period. The Total plan is ¥10,500/year + tax in the first year and ¥21,000/year + tax from the second. The first-year prices likewise cover applications made by March 15, 2027 and require registering a payment method.
:::

:::pull
For the lowest plan that can file a consumption-tax return, the annual price before tax is ¥11,800 for Yayoi's Self plan (free in the first year) and ¥15,360 for Money Forward's Personal plan, which becomes ¥22,560 from December 1, 2026.
:::

Yayoi's first-year price is what applies when the campaign conditions are met. The regular annual fee starts in year two, so the "from the second year" column is the closer picture when comparing. With Money Forward, following the example in the notice, a contract taken on annual billing before December 1 moves to the revised fee at its renewal one year later.

## What actually differs

:::fact
These are the differences that could be confirmed on the two companies' official pages.

| Item | Money Forward Cloud Tax Return | Yayoi Blue Return Online |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Automatic statement import | Bank and credit-card statement import on every plan. "Links with more than 2,300 services" (company survey, February 2024) | Financial-institution links on every plan. More than 1,100 institutions nationwide and more than 2,500 services (as of August 2025) |
| e-Tax | Electronic filing on every plan. Filing from the app requires a My Number card and a smartphone that can read it | e-Tax on every plan. After preparing the filing data, the user launches a dedicated e-Tax service and signs electronically with a smartphone app to submit. Exporting e-Tax data is also supported |
| Smartphone app | The product page describes filing electronically "straight from the app" | The "Yayoi Shinkoku" app and the "Yayoi Receipt Capture" app handle transaction entry and receipt capture. The tax return and the blue-return financial statement are prepared on Windows or Mac and cannot be prepared in the app |
| Consumption tax and invoice system | Preparing a consumption-tax return requires Personal or above. Not available on Personal Mini | Every plan covers the consumption-tax return, invoice-aware journal entry, and the 20% special rule |
| Receipt scanning | Free allowance of 15 per month on Mini, 30 on Personal, 100 on Plus. Beyond that, ¥20 each (before tax); Personal and Plus have no limit from December 1, 2026 | No per-plan limit is stated on the pricing page |
| Email and chat | Available on every plan (how to operate the product) | Basic and above. Self has web FAQ only |
| Phone | Personal Plus only. Covers how to operate the product; journal-entry and tax questions are not accepted | Basic and above. Basic is limited to 10 calls during the support contract period |
| Bookkeeping and filing advice | No plan with this is listed on the pricing page | Total adds journal-entry, bookkeeping, and tax-return consultation |
| Services that come along | 12 services, including invoicing, expenses, payroll, and contracts, are included in the base fee | Links with the cloud invoicing service Misoca, which can be applied for at the same time. Every plan includes a welfare-benefit service |
:::

Both companies say that filing a blue return through e-Tax brings the special blue-return deduction to ¥650,000. Yayoi adds that it supports the "superior electronic books" requirement, one of the conditions for the ¥750,000 deduction that takes effect from the 2027 tax year. Money Forward's pricing page also carries an FAQ saying the MCP server feature, which lets AI tools read and operate the accounting data, costs nothing extra.

Some points could not be confirmed. The two companies count linked financial institutions differently and at different dates, so the numbers alone cannot be compared. Whether a particular bank or card is covered has to be checked on each company's official list. Whether Yayoi White Return Online can file a consumption-tax return could not be confirmed from the pricing page examined here.

## Who each one suits

This is not a ranking. From the facts above, here is which side tends to match each situation.

:::guess
- **Registered invoice issuers who want to keep costs down** appear likely to find Yayoi's Self plan a match. It covers the consumption-tax return at ¥11,800/year + tax from the second year, with the first year free. Support, however, is web FAQ only.
- **People who want to ask by chat or email when stuck** face a split. Money Forward includes email and chat even on Personal Mini, its lowest-priced plan. At Yayoi that starts with Basic (¥22,800/year + tax from the second year).
- **People who want advice on bookkeeping or filing itself** appear to match Yayoi's Total plan. Money Forward states that its phone support covers how to operate the product and excludes journal-entry and tax questions.
- **People who want to finish filing on a smartphone alone** seem closer to what Money Forward describes. Yayoi says the return is prepared on Windows or Mac.
- **People who want invoicing, expenses, and payroll under the same contract** have Money Forward as a candidate, with 12 services in the base fee. If invoicing alone is enough, Yayoi also links with Misoca.
- **Tax-exempt businesses that do not need a consumption-tax return** see nearly the same price band: Money Forward's Personal Mini (¥10,800 on annual billing, unchanged after the revision) and Yayoi's Self (¥11,800 + tax from the second year). Here the decision probably turns less on price than on whether email and chat are needed and whether the institutions in use are covered.
- **White-return filers** can use the Free plan of Yayoi White Return Online at no charge. Support starts with Basic.
:::

## What would prompt a switch

:::fact
According to the FAQ on Yayoi's pricing page, journal data exported from another company's filing software (CSV) can be imported. According to Money Forward's notice, the revised fees apply from contracts renewed on or after December 1, 2026, while the removal of the AI-OCR option charge applies to usage from that date regardless of the renewal timing.
:::

:::guess
Three moments appear likely to prompt a switch. The first is becoming a taxable business by registering as an invoice issuer: at Money Forward this means moving from Personal Mini to Personal, while at Yayoi the plan stays the same. The second is a Money Forward Personal renewal date falling on or after December 1, 2026: annual billing goes from ¥15,360 to ¥22,560, so some users will presumably review their terms before that date. The third is the end of Yayoi's first year: when the renewal email arrives, the user judges whether the regular annual fee is worth it. Conversely, for people who scan many receipts, Money Forward's revision removes the count limit and may be a reason to stay.
:::

## Why the price lists are cut differently

This is the part this site is here for. The two companies sell the same thing, tax-return software, yet they place the price difference in different spots.

:::fact
Money Forward's price list separates plans by features. Consumption-tax returns, bank-balance reconciliation, revenue and expense reports, the number of stored documents (up to 1,000 on Mini), and the free receipt-scanning allowance all vary by plan, while email and chat support comes with every plan. Yayoi's list is the reverse: features are the same on every plan, and plans are split into three tiers of web FAQ, operating questions (phone, email, chat), and business consultation.
:::

:::guess
The difference is presumed to reflect what each company treats as its cost. Money Forward puts 12 services, including invoicing and expenses, into one base fee. Tax filing appears to be the entrance, with the design leading users to higher plans as the business grows and needs consumption-tax returns and other functions. Yayoi keeps software features uniform and charges only for support answered by people. That reads as a design that puts price where labor cost sits and keeps the software itself low-priced. The entrances differ too. Money Forward offers a one-month free trial that does not roll into billing automatically. Yayoi makes the first year free but asks for a payment method and connects it to automatic renewal in year two. One appears to be a short trial followed by a switch to paid, the other a full year of use followed by a move to the regular price.
:::

The business figures and the tech stack of each company are covered in the [Money Forward Cloud article](/en/articles/moneyforward-cloud) and the [Yayoi article](/en/articles/yayoi).

Lining up the annual figures alone does not show how the two differ, because one company prices features and the other prices human support. Is the need a consumption-tax return, a channel for asking questions, or the convenience of finishing on a phone? Deciding that first decides which row of the price list to read.
