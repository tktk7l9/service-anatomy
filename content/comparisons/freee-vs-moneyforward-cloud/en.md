[freee](/en/articles/freee) and [Money Forward Cloud](/en/articles/moneyforward-cloud) are two products that Japanese sole proprietors often weigh against each other when they want to do their annual income tax return (kakutei shinkoku, filed every February to March) in the cloud. This comparison is written for someone who has not signed up yet: it lays out what each company's official pages say, item by item. Every number was checked on the official pages on October 1, 2026; none of it is a hands-on impression. Prices and features change, so check the official pages again before you sign up.

## Prices and plans — both sides list prices excluding tax

:::fact
According to freee's official pricing page, freee Accounting for sole proprietors comes in four plans, and the page states that all listed prices exclude tax. According to Money Forward Cloud Tax Return's pricing page, there are three plans for sole proprietors, also listed excluding tax. The two tables below copy what those pages say. (Japan's consumption tax is added on top at billing.)
:::

freee Accounting (sole-proprietor plans, tax excluded)

| Plan | Annual billing | Monthly billing | Consumption tax return | Support |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starter | ¥980/month (¥11,760/year) | ¥1,780/month | Not included | Chat and email |
| Standard | ¥1,980/month (¥23,760/year) | ¥2,980/month | Included | Chat and email (priority) |
| Premium | ¥39,800/year (annual only) | None | Included | Phone support, tax-audit support |
| Nyuryoku Omakase (data entry outsourced) | ¥49,800/year (annual only) | None | Included | Everything in Premium plus outsourced data entry and journalizing |

Money Forward Cloud Tax Return (tax excluded, prices as of October 1, 2026)

| Plan | Annual billing | Monthly billing | Consumption tax return | Support |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Personal Mini | ¥900/month (¥10,800/year) | ¥1,280/month | Not available | Email and chat (how to operate) |
| Personal | ¥1,280/month (¥15,360/year) | ¥1,680/month | Included | Email and chat (how to operate) |
| Personal Plus | ¥35,760/year (annual only) | None | Included | Phone support (how to operate) |

:::fact
freee's data-entry plan includes 60 items a month in the base price; from the 61st item, a usage fee of ¥70 per item (tax excluded) applies. Plans bought inside the smartphone app have a separate price list: the Help Center lists, tax included, Starter at ¥1,960 monthly or ¥12,800 annually, Standard at ¥3,280 monthly or ¥26,800 annually, and Premium at ¥44,800 annually. Money Forward also has an app-contracted "Personal Mini App Plan," listed tax included at ¥1,500 per month.
:::

Note the different bases: web prices are shown excluding tax, in-app prices including tax. The same plan name shows a different number depending on where you sign up.

## On December 1, 2026, Money Forward's prices change

For anyone who starts preparing their return at year-end, this is the point with the biggest impact.

:::fact
According to the notice on Money Forward Cloud's support site, the base prices for sole proprietors are revised on December 1, 2026 (prices exclude tax; only the Personal Mini App Plan is tax included).

- Personal Mini: monthly ¥1,280 → ¥1,680. The annual price of ¥10,800 does not change
- Personal: monthly ¥1,680 → ¥2,880; annual ¥15,360 → ¥22,560
- Personal Plus: annual ¥35,760 → ¥38,160
- Personal Mini App Plan: monthly ¥1,500 → ¥1,880 (tax included)

For existing contracts, the new prices apply from the contract that renews on or after December 1, 2026. As an example, the notice explains that an annual Personal plan contracted on November 15, 2026 moves to the new price at its renewal on November 15, 2027. At the same time, the option fee for the "enter from AI-OCR" feature that reads receipts and similar documents (¥20 per item from the 31st item a month on Personal and from the 101st on Personal Plus) is abolished for Personal and Personal Plus for usage from December 1, 2026, removing the item limit.
:::

:::pull
For the plans that can file a consumption tax return, the annual price is ¥23,760 on freee Standard and ¥22,560 on Money Forward Personal after the revision. Both exclude tax; the gap is ¥1,200.
:::

Before the revision, the annual prices of the consumption-tax-capable plans were ¥8,400 apart (¥23,760 versus ¥15,360). After it, they are nearly level for anyone who files consumption tax. For those who do not need a consumption tax return, the annual entry points are ¥11,760 for freee Starter and ¥10,800 for Money Forward Personal Mini (unchanged by the revision), so that relationship stays the same.

## What the free period covers, and what remains afterwards

:::fact
freee's official page says the product can be tried free for 30 days with no credit card registration. According to the Help Center, during the trial the tax-return document feature is available "up to entering the return's contents"; output is a paid-plan feature. When the trial period passes, the account switches automatically to a "no plan" state, in which viewing and editing transaction data, creating tax-return documents, and syncing accounts are all listed as unavailable (statement data already imported stays stored). The data-entry plan has no trial.

Money Forward offers a one-month free trial with features equivalent to the Personal plan. According to the pricing page, no credit card is needed and there is no automatic move to a paid plan. According to the support site, it is open to businesses with no past paid contract, once per business. During the trial you cannot preview the tax return, output the PDF or XTX files, or submit. When the period ends the contract status becomes "no contract," and the data you registered is kept.
:::

On both sides, the free period is for keeping books and getting a feel for the screens; outputting or submitting the return is not part of it. Either way, filing requires a paid plan.

:::fact
According to Money Forward's smartphone app page, salaried people can use the app for free to file for the medical expense deduction or furusato nozei (hometown tax donations), or to file side-business income. A sole proprietor needs a paid plan to create and output financial statements.
:::

How far books can be viewed or edited in Money Forward's "no contract" state could not be read from the official pages checked for this piece.

## Where the difference shows up in practice

### The bookkeeping model — entering "transactions" or entering journal entries

:::fact
According to freee's Help Center, freee's basic input form is the "transaction" (torihiki), and you do not need to enter it in the debit/credit form of double-entry bookkeeping. You choose an account (bank, cash, card) and an account title, and register it with a settlement status of "settled" or "unsettled." The help page describes the two big differences from a journal entry as "no need to record debits and credits at registration" and "having a settlement status." It also says that journal entries that cannot be entered in the transaction form are entered as a "transfer slip."

According to Money Forward Cloud Tax Return's support site, there are four screens for registering journal entries by hand: transfer slip entry, simple entry, journal book entry, and entry from transactions. In addition there is "enter from linked services," which builds journal entries from bank and card statements.
:::

:::guess
How this difference lands appears to depend on bookkeeping experience. For someone not used to debits and credits, freee's form — answering whether it is income or expense, and whether it has been paid yet — is likely easier to get into. For someone who has kept books in a journal or with transfer slips, or who exchanges journal entries with a tax accountant, Money Forward, which has several screens close to the conventional ledger form, presumably requires less relearning. That said, freee also has transfer slips and Money Forward also has "simple entry," so neither restricts you to a single input method.
:::

### Automatic import of statements

:::fact
freee's pricing page lists bank and credit card statement retrieval as a feature common to all plans, described as automatically retrieving statement data from more than 1,000 supported accounts and services. Money Forward's service page describes linking with more than 2,300 services including banks and credit cards, and says data can also be entered from household-budget data by linking with its consumer app Money Forward ME.
:::

The two companies do not necessarily count supported services the same way, so comparing the raw numbers is not appropriate. What needs checking is whether the banks, cards and payment services you use for business are supported — which you can confirm by actually setting up the links during the free period.

### E-filing (e-Tax) and the smartphone app

:::fact
freee's official page says both the web version and the app support e-filing through e-Tax (the National Tax Agency's online filing system), and that the app can e-file using a My Number Card (Japan's national ID card). Apps are provided for iOS and Android, and the page says registering transactions and creating and submitting tax-return documents can be done on a smartphone alone.

Money Forward's app page says the app can do journal entries, tax-return creation and e-filing. E-filing from the app requires a My Number Card, a smartphone that can read the card, and a taxpayer identification number for e-Tax. Consumption tax returns can only be created in the web version; one created there can be e-filed from the app.
:::

Both can e-file from the app. Whichever you choose, check beforehand that your phone can read a My Number Card.

### Consumption tax returns and the invoice system

:::fact
freee supports "consumption tax return (invoice system compliant)" on Standard and above. Money Forward supports it on Personal and above, and the Personal Mini plan is explicitly marked as unable to use consumption tax filing. Creating quotes and invoices is listed as a feature common to all freee plans. Money Forward's pricing page says services such as invoicing are included in the base price.
:::

A sole proprietor registered as a qualified invoice issuer under Japan's invoice system has to file a consumption tax return. In that case the plan to pick is Standard or above on freee and Personal or above on Money Forward.

### Support, and what the price includes

:::fact
On freee, chat and email support are common to all plans, with priority handling on Standard and above. Phone support starts at Premium, which also includes tax-audit support (up to 30 hours) and outsourced data migration and initial setup from other software. Receipt capture and upload is up to 5 a month on Starter and up to 10 GB a month on Standard and above.

On Money Forward, email and chat support come with every plan, and phone support only with Personal Plus. According to a note on the pricing page, phone support covers how to operate Money Forward Cloud Tax Return; consultations on journal entries or tax matters are not accepted. The pricing page says that, besides the tax return product, "12 services are included in the base price," such as invoicing, expenses, payroll and contracts. The MCP server feature for operating the books from AI tools also carries no additional charge. Payment is by credit card only; there is no cancellation fee or minimum term, and no refund if you cancel midway.
:::

On operation from AI tools, as covered in [the freee dissection](/en/articles/freee), freee also publishes an official MCP server. That point is common to both.

## Which fits whom — sorted by situation

This is not a ranking. It lines up, situation by situation, what can be read from the official pages.

- No consumption tax return needed (a tax-exempt business not registered for invoices): the annual entry points are ¥11,760 for freee Starter and ¥10,800 for Money Forward Personal Mini (both tax excluded), a small gap. The receipt-reading allowance (5 a month on Starter, 15 a month on Personal Mini) and your preference in bookkeeping form are more likely to decide it.
- Filing consumption tax: freee Standard and Money Forward Personal are the relevant plans. After the December 1, 2026 revision the annual prices are nearly level, so the choice comes down to everything other than price.
- Not used to bookkeeping: freee's "transaction" form, which does not make you think in debits and credits, may fit.
- Used to journal entries, or working with a tax accountant in journal entries: Money Forward, with journal book entry and transfer slip entry, may fit. If you have a tax accountant, asking which software they support is the quickest route.
- Want to ask by phone: phone support comes with freee Premium (¥39,800 a year) and Money Forward Personal Plus (¥35,760 a year, ¥38,160 after the revision). The scope differs: Money Forward states that its phone support covers how to operate the product.
- Want to hand off the data entry itself: among the pricing pages checked for this piece, only freee's data-entry plan lists outsourced entry and journalizing as a plan for sole proprietors.
- Want invoicing, expenses and payroll under the same contract: Money Forward says 12 services are included in the base price.
- Already using Money Forward ME for household budgeting: according to the service page, linking with Money Forward ME lets entries be made from the household-budget data.

:::guess
If you cannot decide, the most reliable comparison is probably to use both free periods and look at two things: whether your own bank and card statements can be imported, and whether the screen for registering those statements makes sense to you. In many situations the price difference stays within a few thousand yen a year, and the difference in time spent on monthly bookkeeping can plausibly be larger.
:::

## When you think about switching

:::fact
Money Forward Cloud Tax Return's support site has a guide for a "freee journal import" feature that takes in journal data exported from freee. freee's Help Center has a procedure for reshaping journal-form data exported from other products into freee's format and importing it as CSV, with a file limit of 64 MB and 50,000 rows. freee's Premium plan includes outsourced data migration and initial setup. Money Forward's pricing page likewise says an onboarding support plan that handles data migration and initial setup is available (no price is given on that page).
:::

Changes that can prompt a switch include:

- Moving from tax-exempt to taxable status, so that you need a plan that can file consumption tax
- A price change at contract renewal (on Money Forward, from renewals on or after December 1, 2026)
- Taking on a tax accountant and aligning with the software they use
- Wanting to bring non-accounting work such as invoicing or payroll into the same service

There is a way to move journal data in either direction. Switching mid-year, however, means reconciling opening balances and sub-accounts, so the period right after filing is likely the easier time to move.

## Where the difference in pricing comes from

:::fact
According to freee K.K.'s FY2026/6 results presentation, paying sole-proprietor users numbered 448,155 at the end of March 2026 and 419,957 at the end of June. Annual ARPU per sole-proprietor user was ¥20,995, and the material says product improvements and pricing optimization raised the share of annual contracts to about 70%. According to Money Forward's FY2026/11 Q2 supplemental data, paying sole-proprietor customers numbered 237,328 at the end of May 2026, and sole-proprietor ARPA was ¥13,165.
:::

:::guess
The two companies' metrics are not defined identically and cannot be compared directly, but there appears to have been a gap in annual revenue per sole proprietor. freee's pricing page puts a large difference on annual billing ("about 45% off" and "about 33% off"), which presumably is a design that uses annual contracts to smooth out the seasonality of cancellations in spring, once filing is over. Its plan steps also stack labor-intensive elements on top: support priority, phone, tax-audit support, outsourced data entry.

Money Forward has split its plans on a single point — whether consumption tax can be filed — and included 12 services in the base price. The December 2026 revision brings the annual price for those who file consumption tax close to freee Standard, removes the per-item reading fee, and leaves annual billing for tax-exempt businesses untouched. It reads as an adjustment that keeps the low entry point while raising revenue per taxable business.
:::

As a result, for a sole proprietor who files consumption tax, the annual prices of the two will barely differ. What remains is on the usage side: whether you think of bookkeeping as transactions or as journal entries, how much you would pay for phone support or outsourcing, and where you want your non-accounting work to live. After lining up the price tables, the thing to look at is less the difference in yen than which design sits closer to how you work.
