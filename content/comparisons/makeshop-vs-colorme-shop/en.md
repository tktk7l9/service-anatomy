[makeshop](/en/articles/makeshop) and [ColorMe Shop](/en/articles/colorme-shop) are Japanese services for building your own e-commerce site on your own domain, and both are run by companies in the GMO Internet Group. Laying the two dissections on top of each other, the difference shows less in the number of cart features than in whether there is a free entrance, how the monthly tiers are cut, and how card payment fees are lowered.

This article was put together by rereading both services' official pages on October 13, 2026. This site has not opened shops on both to compare sales or how they feel in use, so it does not rank them on that. All prices include tax.

## Company and scale: makeshop since 2004 and ColorMe Shop since 2005

:::fact
According to GMO MAKESHOP Co., Ltd.'s company outline and history (as of 2026-10-13), the company was founded on September 22, 2004, launched "MakeShop" in earnest on November 1, 2004 with a 500-yen-a-month plan, and formed a capital alliance with GMO Internet Group, Inc. on May 10, 2005. According to an April 22, 2026 press release, makeshop's gross merchandise value for 2025 was 380.4 billion yen (110.9% of the previous year), and the company claims the No. 1 annual gross merchandise value among Japanese e-commerce SaaS for the 14th straight year. The pricing page says it is used by more than 12,000 shops. According to GMO Pepabo, Inc.'s earnings presentation for Q2 FY2026 (August 13, 2026), ColorMe Shop launched in February 2005 and had 47,075 contracts at the end of June 2026; first-half 2026 revenue was 1,090 million yen (100.9% of the same period a year earlier) and operating income 447 million yen (107.1%), and the presentation attributes the profit increase to a rise in the number of high-margin upper plans. GMO Pepabo's company overview lists its major shareholder as GMO Internet Group, Inc. and others.
:::

| Item | makeshop | ColorMe Shop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operator | GMO MAKESHOP Co., Ltd. | GMO Pepabo, Inc. |
| Started | November 2004 (launched in earnest with a 500-yen-a-month plan) | February 2005 |
| Published scale | Gross merchandise value of 380.4 billion yen in 2025; used by more than 12,000 shops | 47,075 contracts (end of June 2026) |

The published figures are gross merchandise value and shop counts on one side and contract counts on the other, so their sizes cannot be compared.

:::pull
Neither takes a sales commission. The difference is whether there is a free entrance, and how much the upper tiers are asked to pay.
:::

## Pricing tiers: four steps from free at ColorMe Shop, two flat tiers at makeshop

:::fact
According to ColorMe Shop's plans and pricing page (checked 2026-10-13), the sales commission is 0 yen on every plan, and the four plans are Free (no setup or monthly fee), Regular (4,950 yen a month, 3,300-yen setup fee), Large (9,595 yen a month, 3,300-yen setup fee) and Premium (from 35,640 yen a month, 22,000-yen setup fee; the same page's comparison table lists a monthly fee of 39,600 yen). Regular and Large come with a 30-day free trial. The Free plan page gives payment fees from 6.6% plus 30 yen and describes the plan as suited, among others, to shops with monthly sales of 100,000 yen or less. A September 9, 2026 notice cites higher infrastructure costs from rising prices and a weaker yen and revises the monthly fees from November 1, 2026: Regular to 5,940 yen and Large to 11,000 yen, with no change to Premium. According to makeshop's pricing plans (checked the same day), the sales commission is 0 yen; the Premium plan costs 13,750 yen a month with an 11,000-yen setup fee, holds up to 10,000 products and comes with a 15-day free trial; the Enterprise plan costs from 55,000 yen a month with a setup fee from 11,000 yen and holds up to 50,000 products. Long-term contract discounts take up to 15% off the monthly fee. Asked about the downsides of adopting makeshop, the comparison page's FAQ answers: "Because the monthly fee is flat, small shops just after launch may feel it as a fixed-cost burden."
:::

| Tax included | makeshop | ColorMe Shop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sales commission | 0 yen | 0 yen |
| Free tier | None | Free plan (no setup or monthly fee; payment fees from 6.6% plus 30 yen) |
| Middle tier | Premium: 13,750 yen a month, 11,000-yen setup fee | Regular: 4,950 yen a month (5,940 yen from November 1); Large: 9,595 yen a month (11,000 yen from the same date); 3,300-yen setup fee for both |
| Upper tier | Enterprise: from 55,000 yen a month, setup fee from 11,000 yen | Premium: from 35,640 yen a month, 22,000-yen setup fee |
| Free trial | 15 days on Premium | 30 days on Regular and Large |

:::guess
ColorMe Shop welcomes small shops with a free plan and builds a staircase on which they move up as their monthly sales targets grow. Taken together with the earnings presentation crediting the profit increase to more upper-plan contracts and the November price rise for Regular and Large, it appears to be strengthening a model that keeps the entrance wide and earns on the upper tiers. makeshop has no free tier and its own comparison page admits that a flat fee can be a burden for small shops, which reads as pricing aimed at shops that already sell. After the November revision, the gap between ColorMe Shop's Large (11,000 yen) and makeshop's Premium (13,750 yen) narrows to 2,750 yen (this site's calculation).
:::

## Payment fees: makeshop lowers the rate with a monthly fee, ColorMe Shop lowers it by tier

:::fact
According to makeshop's payment page (checked 2026-10-13), card payments cost a 1,650-yen monthly fee and a fee from 3.19% on the Premium plan, and no monthly fee and 3.14% on the Enterprise plan. Amazon Pay costs 3.9% and PayPay 3.45%. According to ColorMe Shop's plans and pricing page, the card payment fee is from 3.4% on Regular, from 3.19% on Large and from 2.99% on Premium. Amazon Pay costs 3.9% on every plan; PayPay costs a 2,200-yen monthly fee plus 3.45% on Regular and Large and 3.45% on Premium; deferred payment is from 4.0% and convenience-store payment from 165 yen.
:::

| Payments (fees) | makeshop | ColorMe Shop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Credit cards | Premium: 1,650 yen a month plus from 3.19%; Enterprise: no monthly fee plus 3.14% | Regular from 3.4%, Large from 3.19%, Premium from 2.99% |
| Amazon Pay | 3.9% | 3.9% |
| PayPay | 3.45% | 2,200 yen a month plus 3.45% on Regular and Large; 3.45% on Premium |

:::guess
makeshop lowers the card rate in exchange for a fixed monthly fee, while ColorMe Shop lowers it as a shop moves to a higher plan; both are built so that payment costs get lighter for shops with larger sales. The 3.9% for Amazon Pay is the same at both, which appears to separate the parts where payment-processor terms leave little room for difference from the parts where each company competes with its own payment service.
:::

## Entry points for AI agents: MCP servers installed as apps

:::fact
According to GMO Pepabo's March 9, 2026 press release, ColorMe Shop began offering the "AI Connector," a remote MCP server, that day. Users install it as an app from the app store and connect it to external AI applications to handle product management, order management and other tasks through conversation. The release calls it the first remote MCP server among Japanese e-commerce cart services (a comparison of seven general-purpose ASP carts offered in Japan, as of March 5, 2026, by the company's own research). According to GMO MAKESHOP's May 11, 2026 announcement, makeshop released "MCP Server for Backoffice," which connects AI agents with a shop's own e-commerce data, on its app platform, makeshop apps; at launch it supports searching orders, members and products. The August 25 press release says official agent skills for shop operations were released as open source on GitHub.
:::

:::guess
Both companies have MCP servers installed through their own app platforms, which appears to put connections on a mechanism where each shop controls what it allows. That ColorMe Shop started in March with product and order management while makeshop started in May with search reads as a difference in the order in which each widens the range of operations entrusted to AI, starting where results are easiest to check.
:::

## Three shared tech stack items

The tech stack comparison shown below this page is a mechanical match of the two articles' techStack entries. Three items were judged shared, PHP, Amazon CloudFront and Amazon S3; where each is used, and how the rest of the parts differ, is covered in each article's tech stack section.

The question for those choosing comes down to current sales and the fixed costs you can carry. For someone who wants to open for free and try first, or move up tiers in step with monthly sales targets, ColorMe Shop is a candidate. For someone who already has sales and wants to read operating costs from a flat monthly fee and the payment terms, or expects custom requirements and large-scale operations, makeshop is a candidate. What separates the two carts in the same group appears to be less the number of features than the design of which size of shop to welcome at the entrance.
