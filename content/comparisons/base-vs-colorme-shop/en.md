[BASE](/en/articles/base) and [ColorMe Shop](/en/articles/colorme-shop) are both online-shop builders run by Japanese companies, and both have a plan that costs 0 yen a month. When an individual or a small business opening a first shop compares the two, the word "free" alone does not show the difference. Reading the official pages side by side, what differs is the path the money from a sale takes: where it passes through, when it reaches the shop, and what is deducted on the way.

This article is written only from what could be confirmed on both companies' official pages and help pages on October 3, 2026. This site has not signed up for or used either service, and it does not rate how they feel to use. Prices and terms change, so check the official pages before you sign up.

## The prices side by side (as of October 3, 2026)

:::fact
According to BASE's pricing page, there are two plans, Standard and Growth. Standard has no initial fee and no monthly fee; when something sells, a payment processing fee of 3.6% + 40 yen and a service fee of 3% are charged. Growth has no initial fee and costs 16,580 yen a month (per month when paid annually; 19,980 yen when paid monthly), with a payment processing fee of 2.9% and no service fee. On both plans, 1% is added to the processing fee when the buyer pays with PayPay, Amazon Pay or PayPal. The FAQ says the Growth contract can be 1 month or 12 months, that the 12-month lump sum is "198,960 yen (tax included)," and that a shop can switch between Standard and Growth at any time (a switch to Growth takes effect immediately; a switch to Standard takes effect after the Growth contract period ends). The Help page on the Growth plan writes monthly payment as "19,980 yen (per month, tax included)" and annual payment as "16,580 yen (per month, tax included)," and says the monthly fee can only be paid by credit card. The plans are described as differing only in their fee rates, with no difference in available features.
:::

| BASE | Initial fee | Monthly fee | Fees when something sells |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard | 0 yen | 0 yen | processing fee 3.6% + 40 yen, service fee 3% |
| Growth (paid monthly) | 0 yen | 19,980 yen | processing fee 2.9% |
| Growth (paid annually) | 0 yen | 16,580 yen (198,960 yen a year) | processing fee 2.9% |

:::fact
According to ColorMe Shop's pricing page, there are three paid plans — Regular, Large and Premium — plus a separate Free plan at 0 yen a month. The page notes that "all prices include tax" (Japan's consumption tax), and the sales commission is 0 yen on all three paid plans. Paid plans are contracted for 3, 6 or 12 months. Regular and Large have a 30-day free trial. A shop can move from the Free plan to Regular or above from the admin screen, but cannot move from Regular or above back to the Free plan; to use the Free plan again, it has to create a new account.
:::

| ColorMe Shop | Initial fee | Monthly fee | Card processing fee |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free | 0 yen | from 0 yen | from 6.6% + 30 yen |
| Regular | 3,300 yen | 4,950 yen | from 3.4% |
| Large | 3,300 yen | 9,595 yen | from 3.19% |
| Premium | 22,000 yen | from 35,640 yen (the comparison table's monthly-fee row says 39,600 yen) | from 2.99% |

:::fact
According to the notes on the same page, using a payment service requires a separate contract with a payment processor, and the processing fees in the table are based on the rates under the payment package "ColorMe Payment." The Premium plan page gives the monthly fee as "from 35,640 yen" and explains that longer contracts lower the monthly fee.
:::

:::fact
According to an official notice (2026-09-09), from November 1, 2026 the Regular plan goes from 4,950 yen to 5,940 yen a month and the Large plan from 9,595 yen to 11,000 yen; the Premium plan does not change. A contract that starts before November 1 is at the pre-revision price. For a contract that starts on or after November 1, the pre-revision price applies if the renewal and payment are completed before November 1, and the revised price applies if they are done on or after November 1 (separate deadlines are given for each automatic debit method).
:::

The two tables cannot be compared as they stand. Most of BASE's cost is "a rate on what sells," while most of the cost of ColorMe Shop's paid plans is "a monthly fee." The tax wording also differs. ColorMe Shop's pricing page notes that all prices include tax, but, as shown below, its Help Center describes the Free plan's fee as "tax excluded" in one place. BASE states that the Growth monthly fee includes tax, but no statement of whether the fee rates on its pricing page include or exclude tax was found.

## What is deducted when something sells

:::fact
According to BASE's Help, the processing fee is charged only when an item sells, deducted from each order's total (including shipping), and rounded to the nearest yen. According to the notes on the pricing page, orders placed through the "PAY ID" shopping app carry a processing fee of 3.6% + 40 yen and a service fee of 5.9% per order regardless of plan.
:::

:::fact
According to ColorMe Shop's Help Center, the Free plan uses a "Free-plan-only ColorMe Payment" (a contract with GMO Epsilon) whose terms differ from the version for paid plans. The processing fee is "6.6% + 30 yen (tax excluded)" per order, Amazon Pay is 6.5% + 30 yen, and fees are offset against sales. Credit cards are optional; a shop that uses them must sign up for "3-D Secure (identity verification service)," which costs 1,000 yen a month (tax excluded). The Free plan page writes the processing fee as "from 6.6% + 30 yen" in its table and as "6.6% of the payment amount + 33 yen" in its FAQ.
:::

:::fact
According to its official page, the ColorMe Payment used on paid plans is a payment package provided by GMO Epsilon, and its fees are paid to GMO Epsilon rather than to ColorMe Shop (GMO Pepabo). In the page's table (tax included, for shops on a paid plan), credit cards are 3.4% on Regular, 3.19% on Large and 2.99%–3.19% on Premium; Amazon Pay is 3.9%; PayPay is 3.45% (plus 2,200 yen a month on Regular and Large); and Rakuten Pay is 4% (plus 2,200 yen a month). Use requires a review, and it takes roughly 5 to 24 business days from application to start. The eligibility condition is having applied for or contracted a paid plan, and the page says Free plan users are not eligible.
:::

On BASE, the rate a shop pays basically does not change as it adds payment methods (apart from the extra 1% for PayPay, Amazon Pay and PayPal). On ColorMe Shop's paid plans, rates and monthly fees are set per payment method, so fixed costs build up as a shop adds methods that carry a monthly fee.

According to ColorMe Shop's Help Center, the payment methods available on the Free plan are Amazon Pay, konbini payment, credit cards (five brands), GMO Atobarai and bank transfer to a virtual account; ordinary bank transfer, Japan Post transfer and cash on delivery cannot be used. On paid plans, the list shows that besides ColorMe Payment, a shop can set up cash on delivery, bank transfer and Japan Post transfer, as well as other payment processors (Kuroneko Web Collect, Zeus, NP Atobarai, PayPal and others). The ColorMe Payment page has separate tables for corporations and individuals; the table for individuals has no row for smartphone carrier billing, and card payment and konbini payment are marked "required" for individual contracts. In that table, cash on delivery (Yu-Pack Collect) is from 352 yen per transaction, while the comparison table on the pricing page writes cash on delivery as from 396 yen.
:::

## Until sales money reaches the shop

:::fact
According to BASE's Help, a shop withdraws its sales by making a "payout request." The money reaches the shop's designated account 10 business days (excluding weekends and holidays) after the request, and each request carries a flat transfer fee of 250 yen, plus an administrative fee of 500 yen if the requested amount is under 20,000 yen. A shop can make one request a day, can request once its sales balance reaches 751 yen, and can request up to 1 million yen per request in principle. For speed, "Express Payout" arrives as soon as the next business day for 1.5% of the amount, and "Fastest Payout" as soon as the same day (including weekends and holidays) for 3%; the transfer fee and administrative fee are charged separately on both. Neither can be used on a shop's first request, and they are opened gradually to shops that meet certain criteria. The pricing page says that issuing a BASE Card lets a shop use its sales right away. The deadline for a payout request is the end of the sixth month after the closing month, where the closing date is the end of the month in which the order was marked "shipped"; sales past the deadline are forfeited.
:::

:::fact
According to the ColorMe Payment page, payouts on paid plans are closed at the end of each month and paid at the end of the following month, with the sales from all payment methods paid together. With the optional "early payout service" (2,200 yen a month and a 0.5% fee), payouts are closed at month end and paid on the 15th of the following month. The Free-plan-only ColorMe Payment is also closed at month end and paid at the end of the following month, according to the Help Center. Whether a transfer fee is charged on these payouts was not stated on the ColorMe Shop official pages this site checked.
:::

BASE pays out the amount a shop requests, when it requests it. ColorMe Shop pays out automatically, in one batch per month. The former lets the shop choose when to withdraw, at the cost of a fee per request and a deadline; the latter needs no request, but money from a sale early in a month takes close to two months to arrive.

## Working out a monthly cost (an illustration)

The following is this site's calculation using only the official numbers above. It is not an actual bill. The assumptions are:

- Every order is paid with a card issued in Japan (Visa/Mastercard).
- There are fixed amounts per order (BASE's 40 yen, ColorMe Shop Free's 30 yen), so the calculation needs an order count; it assumes 5,000 yen per order, shipping included. This is an assumption set by this site, not an official number.
- For ColorMe Shop Free, the 1,000 yen monthly 3-D Secure fee, which is described as required for accepting cards, is included (as written, tax excluded).
- Initial fees, fees for apps and options, paid templates and fees for transferring sales money are not included.
- Rates are used as written on the official pages. As shown above, the tax wording is not consistent, so the amounts are not on the same basis. For ColorMe Shop's "from" rates, the lower bound is used.

| Plan | 100,000 yen a month (20 orders) | 500,000 yen a month (100 orders) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BASE Standard | 7,400 yen | 37,000 yen |
| BASE Growth (paid monthly) | 22,880 yen | 34,480 yen |
| BASE Growth (paid annually) | 19,480 yen | 31,080 yen |
| ColorMe Free | 8,200 yen | 37,000 yen |
| ColorMe Regular | 8,350 yen | 21,950 yen |
| ColorMe Regular (after Nov. revision) | 9,340 yen | 22,940 yen |

The arithmetic:

- BASE Standard: 100,000 × 3.6% + 40 × 20 + 100,000 × 3% = 3,600 + 800 + 3,000 = 7,400 yen. 500,000 × 3.6% + 40 × 100 + 500,000 × 3% = 18,000 + 4,000 + 15,000 = 37,000 yen.
- BASE Growth, paid monthly: 19,980 + 100,000 × 2.9% = 19,980 + 2,900 = 22,880 yen. 19,980 + 500,000 × 2.9% = 19,980 + 14,500 = 34,480 yen. For annual payment, replace the monthly fee with 16,580 yen: 19,480 yen and 31,080 yen.
- ColorMe Free: 100,000 × 6.6% + 30 × 20 + 1,000 = 6,600 + 600 + 1,000 = 8,200 yen. 500,000 × 6.6% + 30 × 100 + 1,000 = 33,000 + 3,000 + 1,000 = 37,000 yen. At 33 yen per order, these become 8,260 yen and 37,300 yen.
- ColorMe Regular: 4,950 + 100,000 × 3.4% = 4,950 + 3,400 = 8,350 yen. 4,950 + 500,000 × 3.4% = 4,950 + 17,000 = 21,950 yen. After the revision, replace the monthly fee with 5,940 yen: 9,340 yen and 22,940 yen.

In addition, withdrawing sales money from BASE once a month adds a 250 yen transfer fee (in this illustration every requested amount is 20,000 yen or more, so no administrative fee applies). As noted above, the fee on ColorMe Shop's payouts could not be confirmed, so it is not compared. BASE's Help gives "about 500,000 yen" a month (excluding orders through the PAY ID app) as the level at which the Growth plan becomes the better deal.

:::guess
Within this illustration, at 100,000 yen in monthly sales BASE Standard and ColorMe Shop's Free and Regular plans (including Regular after the revision) land between 7,400 and 9,340 yen, while BASE Growth comes to about 19,000 to 23,000 yen. At 500,000 yen, ColorMe Shop's Regular is in the low 20,000s, BASE Growth is in the 30,000s, and BASE Standard and ColorMe Shop's Free both come to 37,000 yen. While sales are small, plans with no monthly fee cost less; as sales grow, plans that charge a monthly fee to lower the rate cost less. The sales level at which they cross over appears to depend on the order size, the share of non-card payments, and the apps and options a shop uses.
:::

## Campaigns (as of October 3, 2026)

:::fact
ColorMe Shop's pricing page and Premium plan page carry a notice for a "switching campaign." According to the announcement (2026-08-05), it runs from August 5 to December 31, 2026, covers the Premium plan on a 6-month or 12-month contract, and requires switching to ColorMe Shop from another company's own-site e-commerce service during the period, contracting the Premium plan, and applying through the inquiry form or by booking a sales meeting. The benefits are a waived Premium initial fee of 22,000 yen and points worth 50% of the contract period's fees (112,860 yen on a 6-month contract and 213,840 yen on a 12-month contract, tax included). The points can be used for later renewal payments, paid templates and paid options (1 point = 1 yen) and expire on cancellation. The campaign is not applied automatically and may end without notice.
:::

:::fact
No campaign open for applications was found on BASE's pricing page on the day of checking. The top page of the official site still showed a shop-opening campaign offering a 10%-off coupon, with the deadline written as "until 23:59 on September 16, 2026."
:::

ColorMe Shop's campaign requires "switching from another company's own-site e-commerce service," so someone opening a first online shop does not meet the condition. It also covers only the Premium plan, not Regular or Large.

## Plan changes, support, design and invoices

### Plan changes and contract terms

:::fact
BASE's Standard and Growth plans can be switched at any time, and a Growth contract is 1 month or 12 months. The pricing page says a shop can be opened in as little as 30 seconds and that almost all features can be tried for free. ColorMe Shop's paid plans are contracted for 3, 6 or 12 months, and Regular and Large have a 30-day free trial. A shop cannot go back from a paid plan to the Free plan, and moving from Large to Regular, or from Premium to Regular or Large, is possible only at contract renewal.
:::

### Support

:::fact
According to BASE's Help, there is no phone support line, and inquiries are taken through the inquiry form. According to ColorMe Shop's pricing page, the paid plans have email support (target reply times: within 2 business days on Regular, within 1 business day on Large, and within 1 business day from an EC advisor on Premium) and phone support available within Japan only. The Free plan page says support on the Free plan is the "ColorMe AI Chat" and email, with no phone guidance. The support overview page says the AI chat can be consulted at any time, 24 hours a day.
:::

### Design

:::fact
According to BASE's design page, there are 22 free templates and more than 100 including paid ones. Paid templates are a one-time purchase with no monthly fee, and some customization, such as adding design parts, is not available on them. HTML can be edited with the "HTML Editing App"; the Help explains that themes edited with this app do not automatically receive BASE's updates (new features and bug fixes) and that BASE does not provide technical support for HTML or design. According to ColorMe Shop's feature page, there are about 90 design templates, free and paid combined, and HTML/CSS editing is supported. The comparison table on the Free plan page also has a row for HTML/CSS editing.
:::

### Invoices (qualified invoices)

Japan introduced a "qualified invoice" system in October 2023: a business customer can claim a consumption-tax credit only against an invoice that carries the seller's registration number and certain required items.

:::fact
According to BASE's Help, a shop can choose in its settings whether to issue invoices to buyers, and once set, invoices for eligible orders are sent by email. The shop registers its own registration number; BASE's number cannot be used. The page says invoices and delivery slips that meet the qualified invoice system cannot currently be issued. The same page says the invoices BASE issues to shops cover transfer fees, administrative fees, Express and Fastest Payout fees and the Growth plan's monthly fee, among others, while the BASE Easy Payment processing fee and the service fee are in the category for which no invoice is issued. According to ColorMe Shop's feature page, issuing and printing invoices, delivery slips and receipts is free on every plan (bulk printing is not available on the Free plan), and the official feature list includes an app, "Invoice Kudasai for ColorMe Shop," that lets the buyer generate documents including qualified invoices automatically, from 1,100 yen a month.
:::

Whether ColorMe Shop's standard receipts and invoices satisfy the required items of a qualified invoice, such as the registration number, could not be read from the feature page. A shop with many corporate customers or customers who buy on expenses, or a taxable business thinking about input tax credits on its fees, would do well to ask both companies before signing up.

## Where the tech stacks overlap

In the mechanical comparison above, the technologies the two articles' tech stacks share are PHP and Amazon CloudFront. For PHP, both were confirmed in official engineering blogs as the language of each shop's core application. CloudFront was observed by this site on each company's marketing site (likely) and may be separate from how the shops themselves are served. BASE's "AWS" and ColorMe Shop's "Amazon RDS for MySQL," "Amazon S3" and "AWS Direct Connect" are counted as separate items in the mechanical comparison, but both companies use AWS services. The difference is in the layout: BASE's official blog says it runs on AWS and Amazon Aurora, while ColorMe Shop's official blog describes a hybrid setup with the VMs and Kubernetes nodes that run its application on its own OpenStack private cloud and the master database on Amazon RDS. These differences do not show up directly in what a shop pays.

## Why they split this way

:::guess
The root of the difference appears to lie in how each company earns its revenue. As the [BASE dissection](/en/articles/base) showed, BASE's revenue is centered on fees proportional to shops' sales, and its earnings materials cite a rising take rate (revenue divided by payment-based GMV) as the reason revenue grew faster than GMV. A setup in which the buyer's payment passes through BASE Easy Payment and is paid out by BASE fits a design that earns according to the value of transactions. Being able to keep costs at zero in a month with no sales is presumed to follow from a design that charges in the months when things sell.
:::

:::guess
As the [ColorMe Shop dissection](/en/articles/colorme-shop) showed, the pillar of ColorMe Shop's revenue is the monthly fee, the sales commission is 0 yen, and payment fees go to GMO Epsilon. Monthly fees and rates being listed per payment method on its price list appears to reflect that payments are carved out as a contract with a payment processor rather than handled in-house. A design in which the monthly fee weighs less as sales grow is consistent with the company's numbers, which show it raising revenue per shop by having more shops move to higher plans. It is not a matter of one being good and the other bad; what a company earns from is presumed to decide the shape of its price list and its payouts.

## Which situation fits which

No ranking here. From the facts confirmed above, this sorts out which situations tend to match which design.

:::guess
The situations that appear to match BASE's design are these:

- You do not know whether the shop will sell and want months with no sales to cost 0 yen. Standard has no initial fee and no monthly fee; the costs are the fees when something sells and the transfer fee when you withdraw. In the illustration above, Standard has the smallest cost in the table at 100,000 yen in monthly sales.
- You are starting as an individual and want to offer non-card payment methods together. BASE Easy Payment can in principle be used right after applying, and individuals can use carrier billing too. Note that buyers pay a fee for konbini payment and bank transfer.
- You want to decide when to withdraw sales money yourself. For a fee, transfers as soon as the next business day or the same day are available (with conditions on which shops can use them).
- You want to switch plans month by month as sales rise and fall.

The situations that appear to match ColorMe Shop's design are these:

- You can expect a certain level of monthly sales and want to lower the per-order rate even at the cost of a monthly fee. In the illustration above, Regular has the smallest cost in the table at 500,000 yen in monthly sales.
- You want cash on delivery, or bank transfer in which the buyer pays straight into the shop's account. BASE does not allow either; ColorMe Shop's paid plans can set them up.
- You want to ask questions in Japanese by phone. The paid plans include phone support.
- You want sales money paid in one batch per month without making requests. The time until payout is longer than BASE's standard transfer.
:::

ColorMe Shop's Free plan sits on the same side as BASE Standard in costing 0 yen a month, but accepting cards brings the 1,000 yen (tax excluded) monthly 3-D Secure fee, and the available payment methods are limited. Once a shop moves from Free to Regular or above, it cannot go back to Free. Before starting, it is worth checking which payment methods the plan offers and what the fixed cost will be after moving up. BASE Standard also charges 250 yen each time sales money is withdrawn, plus 500 yen when the requested amount is under 20,000 yen. Sales past the request deadline are forfeited, so it is worth planning how often to withdraw and keeping the deadline in mind.

## When switching comes up

:::guess
The moments likely to prompt a move away from BASE are presumed to be when growing sales make the combined service fee and processing fee heavy, and when cash on delivery or phone support becomes necessary. Since switching to Growth within BASE also lowers the rate, the first comparison would likely be against that plan change. The moments likely to prompt a move away from ColorMe Shop appear to be when falling sales make a paid plan's monthly fee heavy (a shop cannot go back to the Free plan), and when reviewing costs after the November 2026 revision raises the Regular and Large monthly fees. In either direction, switching involves moving product data, design and URLs, but this site did not confirm how much work that takes.
:::

Both have a plan at 0 yen a month. What differs is whose payment service the money from a sale passes through, when it reaches the shop, and what is deducted on the way. Deciding first how much you expect to sell each month and how your customers pay decides how to read the price list.
