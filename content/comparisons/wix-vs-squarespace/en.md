[Wix](/en/articles/wix) and [Squarespace](/en/articles/squarespace) are often compared as website builders that let you publish a site by picking a template and adjusting it with drag and drop. Wix was founded in 2006, is headquartered in Tel Aviv and grew by piling up everything from bookings to payments into a "do anything" product; Squarespace was born in a college dorm room in 2003 and has been chosen for its design. Putting the two dissections side by side, their difference in 2026 lies not in the number of features but in where each makes money in the age of AI.

This article is based on the two companies' official pricing pages, help centers, earnings materials, newsrooms, engineering blogs and Google Cloud's case study, re-read on October 7, 2026. This site has not built the same site on both and measured ease of use or loading speed, so it does not judge design taste or which feels better.

## Scale: a company that reports results and one that no longer does

Wix is listed on Nasdaq and reports quarterly. Squarespace went private in 2024, and its figures can no longer be followed in public earnings filings.

:::fact
According to Wix's results release of August 4, 2026, revenue for April–June 2026 was $563.1 million (up 15% year over year) and total ARR at quarter end $1.963 billion (up 15%); its shareholder update puts registered users at nearly 317 million and headcount, after an organizational realignment in early June, at 4,371. According to investment firm Permira's announcement (2024-10-17), its funds closed the acquisition of Squarespace in an all-cash transaction valued at approximately $7.2 billion, ending Squarespace's listing on the New York Stock Exchange. Squarespace's company page puts its team at more than 1,760 and says millions of websites have been created on the platform, and Google Cloud's case study says it serves millions of customers in more than 200 countries and territories.
:::

| Item | Wix | Squarespace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founded | 2006 | 2003 |
| Capital | Listed on Nasdaq (WIX) | Acquired by Permira for about $7.2 billion in October 2024; private |
| Disclosed scale | Revenue of $563.1 million and ARR of $1.963 billion (April–June 2026) | No results disclosed; millions of sites created since launch |
| Users | Nearly 317 million registered users | Millions of customers in 200+ countries and territories |
| Employees | 4,371 (end of June 2026) | More than 1,760 |

:::pull
Wix widens the entrance for free and lets people build apps with AI. Squarespace has no free entrance and holds customers deeper through domains and the flow of money.
:::

## How prices are set: start free, or try and then pay

Both have four paid tiers, but they build their entrances differently.

:::fact
Wix's pricing page (checked 2026-10-07) says you can create your site for free and choose a plan when you are ready, and lists a free plan that needs no credit card alongside four paid plans: Light, Core, Business and Business Elite. Prices and currency vary by location, displayed prices are for yearly subscriptions, and every plan comes with a 14-day money-back guarantee. In this site's browsing environment the displayed prices were labeled as an offer valid only for the initial yearly term, so this article does not list Wix's amounts. Squarespace's pricing page (checked the same day, viewed from Japan) shows Basic at ¥1,180 a month on annual billing, Core at ¥2,080 and Advanced at ¥3,040; there is no free plan, and every site starts with a 14-day free trial that needs no credit card. According to its help center, Squarespace's current plans are Basic, Core, Plus and Advanced; only Basic charges a 2% online store transaction fee, and the digital products fee falls from 7% on Basic to 0% on Advanced.
:::

| Item | Wix | Squarespace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free plan | Yes (no credit card needed) | No |
| How to try | Build on the free plan; paid plans have a 14-day money-back guarantee | 14-day free trial (no credit card needed) |
| Paid plans | Light, Core, Business, Business Elite | Basic, Core, Plus, Advanced |
| How prices are set | Price and currency vary by location; yearly prices displayed | Per billing country (yen when viewed from Japan) |
| Fees on sales | No transaction fees listed on the pricing page | 2% store fee on Basic; digital products fee of 7–0% |

:::guess
Wix appears to widen its base of registered users with a free plan and then convert more of them to paid plans; its scale of more than 300 million registered users presumably reflects how wide that entrance is. Squarespace has no free entrance and asks people to decide within 14 days whether to pay, which appears to narrow its audience to people using it for business from the start. The former can be read as pricing to "get people building first," the latter as pricing to "deal only with people who pay."
:::

## AI: one building its own models, one charging for usage in credits

:::fact
On January 21, 2026, Wix announced Wix Harmony, an AI website builder that combines an AI agent that takes natural-language instructions with visual editing. In the results release of August 4, 2026, CEO Avishai Abrahami mentioned the launch of Base 1, the proprietary LLM of Base44, its AI app builder, and the release of Wix Harmony's own model earlier in the year; the shareholder update says Base 1 launched in late June. According to the earnings slides, Base44 reached $150 million in ARR in early May 2026, and the company expects Base44's non-GAAP gross margin, near zero at the start of the year, to reach about 60% in the second half. According to Squarespace's help center, its AI features use AI credits: Basic gets only a one-time 10 credits, while Core gets 20 a month and Advanced 120 a month (as shown on the pricing page), and credits pay for prompts sent to large language models such as ChatGPT and Gemini. "AI Visibility," launched in July 2026, tracks how AI search answers questions about a business and is limited to English-language accounts. Refresh 2025 brought an expanded Blueprint AI, which builds templates already filled with content for an industry, and Squarespace GPT, available from ChatGPT's store.
:::

| Item | Wix | Squarespace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main AI products | Wix Harmony (sites), Base44 (apps) | Blueprint AI, Beacon AI, AI Visibility |
| Models | Harmony's own model; Base44's own LLM, "Base 1" | Prompts sent to models such as ChatGPT and Gemini |
| Handling usage | Results explain cutting inference costs with its own models | AI credits (one-time 10 on Basic, 20 a month on Core, 120 on Advanced; packs for more) |
| Business born from AI | Base44 at $150 million ARR (May 2026) | A tool that measures visibility in AI search |

:::guess
Wix appears to have made AI the product itself, a "tool for building apps" as its next offering after websites, and placed itself on the side that holds down inference costs with its own models. Squarespace appears to keep AI as tools for its existing website customers and to share the cost of outside models with them through credits. The former can be read as a bet on winning new customers and revenue with AI, the latter as a defense that keeps AI costs contained within the price.
:::

## Money flows and domains

:::fact
According to Wix's results release, Business Solutions revenue for April–June 2026 was $164.7 million (up 14% year over year), of which transaction revenue, mainly from Wix Payments, was $71.5 million (up 12%); the earnings slides put gross payments volume at $3.6 billion (up 3%). The slides define Creative Subscriptions ARR as including revenue from domain registrations. According to the Squarespace Capital announcement (2025-08-26), Squarespace launched its own Squarespace Payments in 2023 and layered Squarespace Capital, lending powered by Stripe, on top of it, and Refresh 2025 added Squarespace Balance, a financial account, and instant payouts. The countries where Squarespace Payments is available, according to its help center, do not include Japan. On domains, Squarespace finished migrating millions of domains from Google Domains in November 2024 and, at Super Bowl LX in February 2026, aired an ad in which Emma Stone struggles because she cannot get her domain. Wix's second-quarter release also mentions Super Bowl investments in the first half of the year.
:::

:::guess
Even with payments volume growing only 3%, Wix appears to be widening "the rails businesses run on" by building up payments and agency-driven revenue on top of site subscriptions. Squarespace appears designed to earn from each customer for a long time, using domains as the entrance and drawing customers deeper into websites, email, payments and lending. Both advertising at the same year's Super Bowl presumably reflects that the easier AI makes it to build a site, the more it is worth to be chosen by brand name.
:::

## Referral programs

:::fact
Wix runs the Wix Affiliate Program on Impact, paying affiliates when a referred new user converts to a Premium subscription, and its results disclose revenue through agencies, freelancers and resellers such as LegalZoom and Vistaprint. Partners revenue for April–June 2026 was $213.8 million (up 17% year over year), but in revising its outlook in June the company said softness in Partners would weigh on bookings. According to Squarespace's help center, the Squarespace Affiliate Program, run on Impact, pays $100–200 when a first-time customer starts a website subscription, and members of Circle, the program for agencies and freelancers, can receive referral payments without using links.
:::

## The tech overlap: Google Cloud, Apache Kafka and Fastly

The tech stack comparison shown below this page is a mechanical match of the two articles' techStack entries. Three items were judged shared, Google Cloud, Apache Kafka and Fastly, and they are used quite differently. Wix's official Site Reliability page says sites run on Google Cloud, AWS and Fastly in addition to its own data centers, and that traffic is rerouted automatically if one provider goes down: a design that spreads across several clouds to ride out any single provider's outage. Squarespace, according to Google Cloud's case study, is partway through moving from its own data centers to Google Cloud, also uses Google Cloud DNS for its domains, and resells Google Workspace: a direction of leaning on one cloud and making full use of its managed services. Apache Kafka, too, plays different roles. At Wix it is the asynchronous backbone connecting more than 4,000 microservices, moved to Confluent's cloud, with a push-based proxy that cut its cost by 30%. At Squarespace it served as the pipe carrying changes captured by Debezium while databases moved from PostgreSQL to CockroachDB without downtime. Fastly is named by Wix's official page as part of its CDN, while for Squarespace it is still this site's inference from response headers.

The question for buyers splits in two. If you want to start building without spending money, or to build sites and even apps by talking to AI, Wix, with its free plan, Harmony and Base44, fits. If you put design quality first and want one company to handle everything from your domain to payments, Squarespace fits. For businesses in Japan, though, the fact that Squarespace Payments is unavailable and AI Visibility is limited to English-language accounts has to be factored in. Whichever you choose, website builders in 2026 are changing from tools for making sites into places where businesses run on top of AI and payments.
