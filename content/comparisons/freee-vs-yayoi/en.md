[freee](/en/articles/freee) and [Yayoi (Yayoi Blue Return Online)](/en/articles/yayoi) are the two names that come up first when a Japanese sole proprietor files a tax return in the cloud. Both import bank and card statements, build the return, and submit it through e-Tax, the National Tax Agency's online filing system. Their feature lists look alike. What differs is how they price, and each company's history shows in that.

Some background for readers outside Japan. Sole proprietors and freelancers file an income tax return every year between mid-February and mid-March. A "blue return" (aoiro shinkoku) is the filing status that requires proper bookkeeping and in exchange gives a special deduction; a "white return" (shiroiro shinkoku) is the simpler status without it. The "invoice system" is the qualified-invoice regime for consumption tax (Japan's VAT) that started in October 2023.

This comparison uses only what the official sites state. Every price and condition was confirmed on the official pages on October 1, 2026; campaigns and prices can change, so check each company's pricing page before signing up. This is an arrangement of public information, not the result of this site using both products side by side.

:::fact
According to a web survey by MM Research Institute, run on March 23–26, 2026 among 15,845 sole proprietors who filed a return for 2025, Yayoi had 54.0% of sole proprietors using cloud accounting software, freee 25.1%, and Money Forward 15.7%. Accounting software users were 40.9% of all respondents, and 51.0% of those users were on software installed on a PC.
:::

## Comparing prices and plans

Start with the numbers on the official pricing pages. freee states that "all displayed prices exclude tax"; Yayoi writes each amount as "+ tax". Neither side's figures below include consumption tax.

:::fact
freee Accounting has four plans for sole proprietors (official pricing page, tax excluded).

| Plan | Annual billing | Monthly billing | Consumption-tax filing | Support |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starter | ¥11,760 per year (¥980 per month) | ¥1,780 per month (¥21,360 per year) | No | Chat and email |
| Standard | ¥23,760 per year (¥1,980 per month) | ¥2,980 per month (¥35,760 per year) | Yes | Chat and email (priority handling) |
| Premium | ¥39,800 per year (annual only) | None | Yes | Adds phone support and tax-audit support |
| Input Omakase (data entry delegated) | ¥49,800 per year (annual only) | None | Yes | Premium, plus entry and journalizing done for you |

The pricing page says a 30-day free trial is available now and that no credit card is needed to register. The page lists no price that is lower only for the first year. Receipt capture on Starter is limited to 5 per month. The base fee of Input Omakase covers 60 items per month; from the 61st item, ¥70 (tax excluded) is charged per item.
:::

:::fact
Yayoi Blue Return Online has three plans (official pricing page, amounts shown as "+ tax"). The pricing page says "the features are all the same".

| Plan | First year | From year two (per year) | Support |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Self | ¥0 (free for one year) | ¥11,800 | Web FAQ |
| Basic | ¥0 (free for one year) | ¥22,800 | Operating questions by phone, email, and chat (phone up to 10 times per contract period) |
| Total | ¥19,800 (half price for one year) | ¥39,600 | Basic, plus journal-entry, bookkeeping, and tax-return consultations |

The first-year prices come from the "first year free campaign", which covers applications made by March 15, 2027 and applies once per business. Using it requires registering a bank transfer or a credit card as the auto-renewal payment method. The pricing page lists annual prices only; no monthly price is listed. Yayoi says the contract can be cancelled on the web until the end of the month before the final contract month, and that a notice email is sent before renewal. A separate free trial plan lets you try the product for up to two months without the closing and filing functions.
:::

:::fact
Yayoi White Return Online, for people who only file a white return, also has three plans (official pricing page, amounts shown as "+ tax"). The Free plan is "free forever" with Web FAQ support. Basic is ¥0 for the first year and ¥11,500 per year after the free period. Total is ¥10,500 for the first year and ¥21,000 per year from the next year. The first-year prices cover applications by March 15, 2027, and the page says every plan has the same features. The pricing page of Yayoi Blue Return Online also answers that the blue-return product can produce a white return.
:::

:::pull
Compare the lowest annual plan that includes consumption-tax filing: Yayoi Self is ¥0 in year one and ¥11,800 from year two; freee Standard is ¥23,760 every year. Both are shown excluding tax.
:::

A simple two-year total from these figures: for someone who does not need consumption-tax filing, freee Starter on annual billing is ¥11,760 twice, or ¥23,520; Yayoi Self is ¥0 plus ¥11,800, or ¥11,800. For someone who does need it, freee Standard on annual billing is ¥47,520, and Yayoi Self is the same ¥11,800. For someone who wants to ask operating questions by phone, freee Premium is ¥79,600 and Yayoi Basic is ¥22,800. All are tax-excluded and are plain sums of the list prices. Keep in mind that Yayoi's ¥0 first year is a one-time price for applications made by the deadline.

Lining up prices alone is not a fair comparison, though, because "support" does not mean the same thing on each side. freee includes human chat and email even on Starter. Yayoi Self has Web FAQ only; asking a person starts at Basic.

## Comparing the bookkeeping model and features

After price, the points that affect daily use.

### How the books are kept

:::fact
According to freee's Help Center, the basic input format for bookkeeping in freee is the "deal" (torihiki), and there is no need to enter a debit/credit journal entry. The user picks an account (bank, cash, card) and an account title, and states whether it is income or expense and whether it is settled or unsettled. A registered deal is automatically converted to and stored in double-entry form, and the journal and general ledger are created at the same time. People used to double-entry can also record directly in journal form through a transfer slip.
:::

:::fact
According to Yayoi's feature list, the entry menu of Yayoi Blue Return Online has three items: "Kantan (easy) transaction entry", "Smart transaction import", and "Journal entry". The official site says that even without bookkeeping knowledge, entering the date and amount is enough for double-entry books to be created automatically. The product can output a cash book, deposit book, accounts receivable and payable ledgers, the journal, and the general ledger. The FAQ on the pricing page, meanwhile, recommends the installed product "Yayoi Blue Return 26" to people who want to keep books carefully with slips and ledgers. That product's official page says entries can be made either from simplified ledgers or from receipt, payment, and transfer slips.
:::

So the tidy contrast "freee hides bookkeeping, Yayoi shows the ledgers" does not hold between the two online products. Both are built so that someone who does not know bookkeeping can start from a date and an amount. The traditional style of entering through ledgers and slips is handled, at Yayoi, by the installed product. What is specific to freee is that a deal carries a settled or unsettled state, which the Help Center says can be used to manage receivables and payables.

### Automatic statement import

:::fact
freee's official site says it automatically retrieves bank and credit card statements and supports more than 1,000 accounts and services, including e-commerce sites, e-money, and POS register services. Yayoi's official site says it can link data with more than 1,100 financial institutions nationwide and more than 2,500 financial-institution services (as of August 2025), retrieving statements automatically and journalizing them with AI.
:::

freee gives one figure for accounts and services together; Yayoi gives separate figures for institutions and for services. The counting methods are not necessarily the same, so the sizes cannot be compared directly. What matters is not the total but whether your own bank and card are supported. Check the official pages before signing up.

### e-Tax and smartphone apps

:::fact
freee's official site says everything from daily work such as registering deals and issuing invoices to preparing and submitting the tax return can be done on a smartphone alone, with submission done by holding a My Number Card (the national ID card) against the phone. Yayoi's official site says filing by e-Tax is completed from Yayoi Blue Return Online, with the electronic signature made in a smartphone app after the return data is prepared. Yayoi's "Yayoi Shinkoku" app is for daily transaction entry; the site states that the tax return and the blue-return financial statement are prepared on a Windows or Mac computer and cannot be prepared in the app.
:::

The difference here is where the return is prepared. freee says the return can be prepared and submitted on a phone alone. Yayoi says transaction entry and receipt capture work on a phone, and the return is prepared on a computer. Both need a My Number Card for e-Tax.

### Invoice system and consumption-tax filing

:::fact
On freee's pricing page, consumption-tax filing that supports the invoice system is a feature of Standard and above and is not included in Starter. Creating quotes and invoices is common to all plans. The FAQ on Yayoi's pricing page answers that consumption-tax filing, the income tax return, invoice-system support, and e-Tax filing are available on every plan, and that all features are ¥0 in the first year. According to Yayoi's official site, Yayoi Blue Return Online supports the 20% special rule for tax-exempt businesses that became registered invoice issuers, and the small-amount special rule for taxable purchases under ¥10,000.
:::

Whether you must file consumption tax depends on whether you are a taxable business. Anyone registered as an invoice issuer is a taxable business. For those people, the choice at freee is Standard or above, while at Yayoi any plan will do. For everyone else this difference does not matter. If you are not sure which you are, asking the tax office or a tax accountant is the reliable route.

### Support and tax-accountant links

:::fact
According to freee's official site, chat and email support are common to all plans; the chat bot answers 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, and human chat is available during business hours. Standard and above get priority handling, and phone support, up to 30 hours of tax-audit support, and delegated data migration from other software come with Premium and above. At Yayoi, Self has Web FAQ, Basic has operating questions by phone, email, and chat, and Total adds consultations on journal entries, bookkeeping work, and the tax return.
:::

:::fact
Yayoi's official site presents its partner program for tax accountants and accounting firms, "Yayoi PAP", and says accounting data can be shared with your tax accountant in real time. The number of accounting firms is given as "more than 14,000" on the tax accountant link page and as "recommended by more than 11,000 accounting firms nationwide" on the product top page; the two pages show different figures. freee runs a site called "freee tax accountant search" where users can look for certified-advisor tax accountants and accounting firms. When this site checked on October 2, 2026, the top page of that site said "more than 2,300 listed firms." Yayoi's figures count firms that recommend it or partner with it, while freee's counts firms listed on its search site; they are counted differently, so the sizes cannot be compared directly.
:::

If you already have a tax accountant, ask which software they can receive data from before you choose. Changing later changes the work on the accountant's side as well.

## Which fits whom

From here on, the facts above are applied to situations. This is not a ranking. The answer changes with what a given person weighs most.

- You do not need consumption-tax filing and want to keep the cost down. freee Starter (¥11,760 per year) and Yayoi Self (¥0 in year one, ¥11,800 from year two) are the candidates. From year two the prices are nearly the same; the differences are the free first year and whether you can ask a person. freee Starter comes with chat and email; Yayoi Self has Web FAQ only.
- You registered as an invoice issuer and need to file consumption tax. Yayoi allows consumption-tax filing on every plan. At freee it starts at Standard. If Web FAQ support is enough, Yayoi Self (¥11,800 from year two) is about half of freee Standard (¥23,760). Yayoi Basic (¥22,800 from year two), which lets you ask operating questions, sits in nearly the same price band as Standard; the difference there is the ¥0 first year.
- You want to ask operating questions by phone. Phone support starts at Basic at Yayoi (¥22,800 from year two, up to 10 calls per contract period) and at Premium at freee (¥39,800). The Yayoi plan without the 10-call cap is Total (¥39,600 from year two), which sits in nearly the same price band as freee Premium.
- Asking in writing is enough for you. freee has human chat and email from its lowest plan. Doing the same at Yayoi takes Basic.
- You have no computer and want to finish the filing on a phone. freee says the return can be prepared and submitted on a phone. Yayoi states that preparing the return needs a computer.
- You want to pay only for the months you need. Monthly billing exists on freee Starter and Standard. Yayoi's pricing page lists annual prices only. Note that freee's monthly billing costs more than annual billing if kept for 12 months.
- You only file a white return. Yayoi has Yayoi White Return Online with a Free plan that is free forever. freee says it also supports white returns.
- You want someone else to do the bookkeeping itself. freee's Input Omakase (¥49,800 per year, up to 60 items per month) does the entry and journalizing for you. As far as this site could confirm, the price list of Yayoi's online product did not show a plan that takes over the entry work itself.
- You want to keep the books yourself in slip and ledger form. Yayoi recommends its installed product. freee has a function for recording in journal form through transfer slips.

### When switching comes up

- When a tax-exempt business becomes a taxable one. Someone on freee Starter chooses between moving up to Standard and moving to another vendor.
- When Yayoi's free period ends. The annual fee starts in year two. Yayoi says it sends an email before renewal and that the contract can be stopped on the web until the end of the month before the final contract month. It also says that after cancelling, the data can no longer be viewed, and that re-contracting is a new contract that cannot take over the data.
- When you want to change how you ask questions. If you come to need the phone, or stop needing it, at Yayoi you change plans within the same product. Adding phone support at freee means moving up to Premium.
- When you start working with a tax accountant. You may end up matching the software they use.

As for the means of switching, Yayoi says it can import journal data (CSV) exported from other vendors' tax-return software, and freee says that on Premium and above it handles data migration from other software and the initial setup for you. Moving in the middle of a year raises the question of how to treat the transactions recorded before the move. Moving at the turn of the year appears to involve less work.

## The structure behind the difference — cloud-born versus a company that came from boxed software

:::fact
According to freee K.K.'s company profile, the company was established in July 2012. According to Yayoi Co., Ltd.'s company information, Yayoi was founded in 1978 and its shareholder is the KKR group. Yayoi still sells "Yayoi Blue Return 26", which is installed on a PC, alongside the online version. According to Yayoi's notice, Yayoi Blue Return Online revised its prices from January 1, 2026: the annual fee (tax excluded) went from ¥10,300 to ¥11,800 for Self, from ¥17,250 to ¥22,800 for Basic, and from ¥30,000 to ¥39,600 for Total.
:::

:::guess
That freee splits plans by features and Yayoi splits them by support appears to come from how each company grew up. freee began as a cloud subscription, and what it sells is the software's features themselves. As a business grows and comes to need consumption-tax filing or permission controls, it moves to a higher plan. That staircase reads as following the standard SaaS practice of raising revenue per user as the user grows. Yayoi, on the other hand, has long sold boxed software plus annual maintenance and support. It is presumed to have carried the installed-software way of dividing things into its online product as is: hand everyone the same software, and put the price on support where a person answers the phone. The fact that the January 2026 revision raised Basic and Total by more than Self is also consistent with the weight of the price sitting on the part that takes human labor.
:::

:::guess
The ¥0 first year appears to reflect the same structure. In the MM Research Institute survey, half of sole proprietors who use accounting software are still on installed software. For Yayoi, the online version is presumably both an entrance for new users and the destination for moving its own installed-software users to the cloud. Making year one free while having the payment method registered works for both. freee has no such installed base to move, so after the 30-day free trial it collects a fee from the first year and discounts annual billing well below monthly billing to steer users into one-year contracts, or so it reads. Both shapes can be seen as different answers to the same circumstance: a tax return happens only once a year.
:::

The difference between the two price lists is a question of which costs less, and it is also a record of what each company has sold as it grew. Does a design where you climb a staircase of features suit your business, or one where the features stay the same and you choose only the help desk? Before the filing season opens in February, settle three things: whether you will file consumption tax, how you want to ask questions and of whom, and which device you will prepare the return on. The two price lists become easier to read after that.
