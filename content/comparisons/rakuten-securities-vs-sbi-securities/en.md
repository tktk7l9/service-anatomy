[Rakuten Securities](/en/articles/rakuten-securities) and [SBI Securities](/en/articles/sbi-securities) look startlingly alike when you lay only the fee tables side by side. Japanese stock commissions are ¥0, US stocks cost 0.495% including tax with a $22 cap, and the buyer's margin rate is 2.80% a year. Years of competition have made the numbers converge. What is worth comparing is less the rates than the conditions for reaching zero, the card and point camps, and how each writes about incidents. This article sets out what both companies' official pages and earnings documents said when we read them on November 2, 2026. We have not opened accounts or used either service. Fees and conditions change, so always check the official pages before applying. Investing can lead to losses through price movements, and this article does not recommend investing.

## Scale and results over the same six months

Rakuten Securities closes its books in December and SBI Securities in March, so full-year figures cannot be compared directly. Rakuten's Q3 notice includes reference figures for April to September 2026, which line up with SBI's interim results.

:::fact
The figures below come from Rakuten Securities' earnings documents and SBI Securities' earnings report and presentation, rearranged by us. Operating margins and multiples are our calculation. Some SBI figures include its group's online brokers, and it also has face-to-face and IPO underwriting businesses, so this is not a comparison of individual online trading alone.

| Item | Rakuten Securities | SBI Securities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operating revenue (April–September 2026, consolidated) | ¥115,660 million | ¥191,013 million |
| Operating income (same) | ¥40,468 million | ¥66,866 million |
| Operating margin (same) | 35.0% | 35.0% |
| Net income attributable to owners of the parent (same) | ¥20,757 million | ¥45,436 million |
| Accounts (end of June 2026) | 14.39 million general accounts | 16.383 million securities accounts |
| Client assets (end of June 2026) | ¥58.7 trillion | About ¥75.3 trillion (¥73.5797 trillion at end of September) |
| Margin loans (end of September 2026) | ¥1.627 trillion | ¥2.641 trillion |
| Margin accounts | 891,548 (end of December 2025) | About 2.044 million (end of June 2026) |
| NISA purchases (January–June 2026) | About ¥4.0 trillion | ¥3.8768 trillion |
| Monthly fund savings | ¥317.1 billion (settings, excluding bonus settings, end of June 2026) | ¥341.2 billion (total of fund savings, SBI Wrap savings and foreign currency MMF savings, end of June 2026) |

SBI Securities' presentation shows the April to June 2026 revenue mix for both companies. Brokerage commissions were 12.1% at SBI and 16.8% at Rakuten, financial income 41.5% and 44.4%, and trading gains 23.7% and 18.6%.
:::

:::guess
The operating margins matching to one decimal place is presumably a coincidence, but the revenue mix also shows how close the two models are. Both earn in the teens from brokerage commissions and in the 40% range from financial income. The two brokers that went free appear to have converged on the same place. The gap shows up in scale. SBI's revenue is about 1.65 times Rakuten's, wider than the roughly 1.28 times in client assets. The weight of trading gains and a business range extending to face-to-face and underwriting are presumed to explain part of that. NISA purchases, meanwhile, are larger at Rakuten, suggesting a contrast between Rakuten's depth in small regular investing and SBI's depth in margin balances.
:::

## The conditions for reaching ¥0

:::fact
Rakuten Securities' official pages (accessed 2026-11-02) say the Zero Course makes internet trading in Japanese stocks (cash and margin) ¥0 regardless of trade size. Use of its designated SOR orders is in principle required (except for orders from designated tools and order types), and setting the course requires understanding and agreeing to SOR and R-Cross, the company's internal trading system, a dark pool. SOR connects to up to four markets: the Tokyo Stock Exchange, JAX PTS, Japannext PTS and R-Cross.

SBI Securities' fee page (accessed 2026-11-02) says that on either the Standard or the Active plan, customers who meet the conditions qualify for the "Zero Revolution" and pay ¥0 on Japanese stock trades regardless of trade size. It states that customers who have not applied for electronic delivery are excluded.

Outside those conditions, the fees are close. Per-trade fees on Rakuten's Super Discount Course are ¥55 up to ¥50,000, ¥99 up to ¥100,000 and ¥1,070 above ¥30 million (all including tax). SBI's Standard plan cash table shows the same amounts on the same rows (¥55, ¥99 and ¥1,070 including tax).
:::

:::fact
Other rates are as follows (all from official pages accessed 2026-11-02).

| Item | Rakuten Securities | SBI Securities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| US stocks (cash, taxable account) | 0.495% of trade value including tax, capped at US$22 including tax | 0.45% of trade value (0.495% including tax), capped at $20 ($22 including tax) |
| Trade value at which the minimum fee is $0 | US$2.22 or less | US$2.02 or less |
| US stock margin trading fee | Free (excluding operator orders) | US$0 |
| Buyer's rate, Japanese stocks (standardized margin) | 2.80% a year (preferential 2.28%) | Normally 2.80% (preferential 2.28%) |
| Buyer's rate, Japanese stocks (negotiated margin) | 2.80% a year (preferential 2.10%) | Normally 2.80% (preferential 2.10%) |
| Same-day margin interest | Ichinichi margin at 0.00% a year | 0% for orders of ¥1 million or more; below that, 1.80% a year on both buyer's interest and stock lending |
| Odd-lot stocks | Kabu Mini is commission-free, with a 0.22% spread on real-time trades and none on opening trades | S-Kabu has no fee on buying or selling |

For NISA, Rakuten's official page advertises free trading in Japanese and US stocks, and SBI's guide also advertises free commissions on US stocks.
:::

:::guess
The difference in conditions appears readable as a difference in what each company wanted. SBI's condition is electronic delivery, which moves customers away from paper documents. Rakuten's is SOR and R-Cross, which puts orders on execution routes it designed. From the user's side, SBI asks only for a decision to receive documents electronically, while Rakuten adds the step of understanding and agreeing to how execution works. Which execution is better varies by stock and time of day, so this comparison cannot say.
:::

## The card and point camps

:::fact
Card investing runs from ¥100 to ¥100,000 a month at both, but rates are set differently.

| Item | Rakuten Securities | SBI Securities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eligible cards | Rakuten Card (Mizuho Rakuten Card also works) | Sumitomo Mitsui Card and others |
| Main points | Rakuten points | V Points (SBI also lets customers choose Ponta, d Points, PayPay Points or JAL miles) |
| What sets the rate | Card type and the fund's agent fee | Card type and annual card spend |
| Entry-level card rate | Rakuten Card 0.5% (1% on funds with an agent fee of 0.4% a year or more) | Sumitomo Mitsui Card (NL) 0.5% with ¥100,000 or more of annual spend, 0% below that |
| Advertised maximum | 2% (Black card), up to 3.2% from January 2027 purchases | Up to 6% (up to 4% by card, plus up to 2% for meeting Olive asset management service conditions) |

Rakuten also offers Rakuten Pay balance payment (up to ¥50,000 a month, 0.5%). The Black card's 3.2% requires ¥4 million or more of shopping spend in the previous year, and card investing in Rakuten Securities funds does not count toward it. At SBI too, card investing is excluded from annual card spend. Rakuten limits balance-based fund points to six funds including the Rakuten Plus series, and others earn only when a balance first reaches set levels. SBI's fund mileage pays by fund, such as 0.1% for ordinary funds (0.2% with a monthly average holding of ¥10 million or more), and its page says a wide range of funds is covered.
:::

:::fact
What points can buy is also described differently. Rakuten's page says Rakuten points can buy funds, Japanese stocks (cash), US stocks (yen settlement) and binary options at one point to the yen, and that buying ¥30,000 or more a month of funds or US stocks with points lifts the Rakuten Ichiba SPU rate. SBI's page says points can be used to buy and regularly invest in funds and Japanese stocks. On bank links, Rakuten's Money Bridge applied 0.58% a year to ordinary deposits up to ¥10 million as of November 2, 2026.
:::

:::guess
What matters in comparing points is less the maximum rate than which row your own card spend lands on. SBI's 6% stacks a high-fee card, annual spend and Olive conditions, and many entry cards earn far less. Rakuten's rate is set regardless of spend on most cards, so light card users are unlikely to fall to 0%. Conversely, people who pay for daily life with a Sumitomo Mitsui Card appear more likely to reach the upper rows at SBI. The card camp you already belong to is presumed to often be the answer in itself.
:::

## Unauthorized access and authentication

:::fact
Rakuten Securities' compensation policy (2025-07-25, updated 2026-02-18) sets out a baseline of 50% of the loss in cash, a refund of trading commissions and a flat ¥10,000 consolation payment. Fraudulent sales of securities already held are excluded from the loss calculation but qualify for the consolation payment. SBI Securities' policy (for cases through 2025/10/25) likewise provides in principle 50% cash compensation, full compensation of costs such as trading commissions and a flat consolation payment, with costs and the consolation payment as the compensation when the damage is limited to sales of securities held. SBI explains that the framework stems from an agreement among ten securities companies published by the Japan Securities Dealers Association on May 2, 2025.

The difference lies in how each treats the period after authentication was strengthened. SBI Securities introduced passkeys on October 25, 2025 and states that, for damage occurring on or after October 26, 2025, it changed its policy to decide whether and how to compensate after examining each case, according to ID and password management and use of security features. Rakuten Securities introduced passkeys across all channels on October 26, 2025 and has been making them mandatory in stages since June 2026, and once a passkey is set the old login can no longer be used alongside it. SBI's FAQ says passkeys are in principle mandatory from June 30, 2026, but that login by username and password remains possible, with prompts shown until a passkey is set.

Rakuten Securities announced on October 31, 2026 that, through unauthorized access at the contractor of an external service provider, information on some customers who bought "digital benefit bonds," 362 cases in total, may have been taken. It says its own systems were not the target.
:::

## Parents and partners

:::fact
Rakuten Securities' shareholders are Rakuten Securities Holdings (51.0%) and Mizuho Securities (49.0%), and the holding company became a consolidated subsidiary of Rakuten Bank on October 1, 2026. SBI Securities' earnings documents list among its main initiatives the start of Olive Consulting's operations, which support asset management on Olive (May 2026), and financial instrument intermediary services with regional banks (banks under Yamaguchi FG, Fukuoka Bank, Kumamoto Bank and Juhachi-Shinwa Bank). Rakuten Securities' main links are inside its group, with Rakuten Card and Rakuten Bank, and with Mizuho Bank and Mizuho Securities.
:::

:::guess
In short, the difference appears to be a closed ecosystem versus an open partner network. Rakuten holds cards, banking, points, e-commerce and mobile inside its group, and as 86.7% of general accounts belonging to Rakuten members shows, it carries existing Rakuten customers into securities. SBI connects widely to other companies' customer bases through cards, points, banks and regional lenders. Still, Mizuho has entered as an outside party on the Rakuten side, and SBI has a particularly close partner in SMBC, so the split is becoming less clean.
:::

## Which conditions fit which kind of use

From here on, this is our reading of the conditions above. It is not a judgment on whether to invest. It shows which rows of the official conditions to read for someone who has already decided to open an account.

:::guess
- People who shop on Rakuten Ichiba and use Rakuten Card and Rakuten Bank are likely to find Rakuten Securities' conditions fit. Rows of 0.5% or more apply regardless of annual card spend, and SPU and Money Bridge work under the same membership.
- People who use Sumitomo Mitsui Card or Olive for daily payments and spend a lot each year are presumed more likely to reach the upper rows of SBI's table. Conversely, those whose annual card spend falls short of ¥100,000 should check that the rate becomes 0%.
- People who hold no Rakuten points or collect others have more freedom with SBI's point choice. Anyone wanting Ponta, d Points or JAL miles would read SBI's guide.
- People holding large margin positions see the same interest rows, so the comparison moves to eligible stocks, negotiated margin inventory and preferential conditions. This article does not compare inventory.
- People who repeat small same-day margin trades may feel the row difference between Rakuten's 0.00% on Ichinichi margin and SBI's 1.80% a year on orders under ¥1 million.
- People who want paper documents fall outside SBI's free condition. People who want to avoid agreeing to the execution route fall outside Rakuten's Zero Course. The fee conditions divide here.
- It is possible to hold general accounts at both. However, Rakuten Securities' NISA page says only one financial institution per person can hold a NISA account opened in the same year, so NISA alone means choosing one.
:::

## Why the numbers ended up so similar

:::guess
The two fee tables appear to match as a result of long competition in which one cut and the other followed. Once fees reach ¥0, what remains is income from balances, and the way to grow it is more accounts and more regular investing. So both invested in cards and points, competed for the NISA entry point and came to rest on margin balances.

The difference is who owns the road carrying balances in. Rakuten has its own road, so part of its costs circulates inside the group. SBI connects to other companies' roads, so it spreads as connections grow. Transaction-related expenses as a share of selling, general and administrative costs were 62.3% at Rakuten in January to June 2026 and 31.2% at SBI in April to September 2026, a large gap, but cost categories may differ by company, so they cannot be compared simply (shares are our calculation). Even so, the gap may reflect how differently the two are put together.
:::

Rakuten Securities and SBI Securities reach the same ¥0 by different routes. With the fee tables now nearly identical, the rows to read when choosing are the condition rows, not the rate rows. Which camp do your everyday payments belong to? Which can you agree to, electronic documents or an execution route? How do you read each company's wording on incidents? Open the official pages with those answers in hand, and whose conditions fit your use should become clear.
