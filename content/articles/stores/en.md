STORES sells an online shop builder, cashless payments, a POS register, and a booking system under one account. They began as separate services from separate startups, gathered under a holding company in 2018, merged into one company in 2021, and only in March 2026 arrived at "one plan." The time between the merger and the unified price list is also the time it took to integrate the systems.

## Service overview

STORES, Inc. is a privately held company headquartered in Ebisu, Tokyo, offering online shop creation, a POS register, cashless payments, online booking, and app creation to small and mid-sized shops. Its incorporation date is March 23, 2012, but that is the founding date of Coiney Corp., which built the Coiney payment service; the STORES.jp online shop builder was started the same year by a different company, Bracket Corp.

:::fact
According to the history on the official company page (as of 2026-10-06), Bracket Corp. released "STORES.jp" on August 30, 2012, Coiney Corp. released "Coiney" on April 10, 2013, and Coubic Corp. released the booking service "Coubic" on April 10, 2014. On February 1, 2018, Coiney and STORES.jp Corp. came together under hey Inc., a newly founded operating holding company. The service brands were unified as "STORES" on January 30, 2020, Coubic joined the group on August 4 of that year, and on January 1, 2021 the three companies were absorbed into hey as the single operating company. "STORES Register" launched on June 15, 2021, hey Inc. was renamed STORES, Inc. on October 1, 2022, Digitar Corp. joined the group on January 20, 2025, and "Makitori by STORES" launched on May 30 of that year. The president and CEO is Yusuke Sato, and revenue is listed as ¥15,625 million for fiscal 2025.
:::

:::fact
According to the official pricing page (as of 2026-10-06), there are two plans. The Free plan costs ¥0 a month, with in-person payment fees from 2.48%, a payment terminal at ¥27,720 (tax included), and basic features only. The Standard plan costs ¥3,300 a month (tax included, annual contract; ¥3,960 on a monthly contract), with in-person payment fees from 1.98%, one terminal lent free of charge on an annual contract, and every feature. Plans apply per shop, not per business, and businesses with ¥30 million or more in annual cashless payments pay 3.24% on Free and 2.98% on Standard for in-person credit card payments. The Standard plan gives no refund for cancellation during the contract term. The bottom of the page says that more than 250,000 shops in total have adopted STORES.
:::

:::fact
According to the STORES Online Shop pricing page (as of 2026-10-06), online shop payment fees start at 5.5% on the Free plan and 3.6% on the Standard plan, while PayPal, Paidy, atone, Rakuten Pay, PayPay balance, Amazon Pay, and carrier billing cost 6.5% and 4.6% respectively. Businesses with monthly sales of ¥1.5 million or more are offered an individual plan from 2.9%. Sales are normally paid out at the end of the month following the month-end close, when the amount after the payment fee and a ¥275 transfer fee is ¥10,000 or more. "Speed Cash," which pays out as early as the next day, costs the ¥275 transfer fee plus 1.5% on Standard or 3.5% on Free. Custom domains are available only on the Standard plan, there are 48 free templates, and 3D Secure 2.0 is supported as standard.
:::

:::pull
For ¥3,300 a month, one contract covers the online shop, cashless payments, POS register, booking, mobile ordering, analytics, and invoice payments. But it applies per shop, and payment fees are charged separately on sales.
:::

::scorecard

## UX analysis

The STORES experience is built around "we take over the whole digitization of your shop." Traces of the days when these were separate services survive less in the pricing page than in the individual fee tables.

- **Two plans, fees by channel.** The monthly fee is a clear choice between ¥0 and ¥3,300. Payment fees, however, differ by sales channel even on the same Standard plan: from 1.98% for in-person credit cards, from 3.6% for the online shop, from 3.3% for booking prepayments, and from 2.98% for invoice payments. And what sits behind each "from" depends on the payment method, the size of the business, and an application-based "small business special rate." The simplicity of the monthly fee and the complexity of the fee tables live side by side.
- **The lowest rate requires an application.** According to the press release of 2026-07-22, the booking prepayment rate from 3.3% applies when a shop is on the Standard plan and has been approved for the "small business special rate," which requires being a small or medium enterprise under Japan's Small and Medium-sized Enterprise Basic Act, less than ¥30 million in annual cashless payments, and an in-person setting such as a physical store. For sign-ups from August 3, the application is filed automatically together with the credit card review and applied in as little as five days after approval. Signing up without reading is unlikely to cost you, but whether the smallest number in the table applies to your shop takes checking.
- **Setup can be handed to people and AI.** According to the press release of 2026-08-03, "Omakase Start" sets up the online shop, booking, mobile ordering, POS register, and payments on the shop's behalf for ¥55,000 (tax included) per shop, delivers in as little as 10 days, and gives 14 days of direct access to the person in charge after delivery. It is available to subscribers of the Standard plan or a paid booking plan, and the company positions it as a new "AI BPO" service.
- **It prepares for rule changes ahead of time.** According to the press release of 2026-07-31, in anticipation of the government's planned cut of the consumption tax rate on food to 1% from April 2027, the company completed development to support the new rate in the POS register, online shop, mobile ordering, and invoice payments, plans to offer bulk and scheduled rate settings across channels, and charges nothing extra for it. Because the details depend on the bill passing the Diet, the company says it will adapt to the final rules.

## Tech stack

::techstack

:::fact
According to the STORES Product Blog post of 2024-11-19, written by an engineering manager, STORES is a company formed by the merger of several startups, each with services that had been developed for more than ten years counting from the first commit. STORES Register and STORES Online Shop share one monolithic Ruby on Rails backend and one database, so product and inventory data are linked, and it is the company's largest Rails repository with more than 1,500 model classes. The online shop frontend is Nuxt, and the same backend exposes APIs for both the iOS app and Nuxt. STORES Reservations is a separate Rails monolith with a Next.js frontend and more than 1,000 model classes. Besides Rails there are Java and Go services, both React and Vue on the frontend, and both AWS and GCP.
:::

:::fact
According to the same post, to unify login accounts across services built by separate startups, the company developed and operates its own OpenID Connect identity platform in Go and Next.js rather than using an IDaaS such as Amazon Cognito or Firebase Authentication. An API Gateway that authenticates and authorizes requests with the identity platform's access token and routes them to the right backend, and a Pub/Sub system that sends webhooks to internal subscribers, were each built almost single-handedly by one engineer. When linking the booking system to the POS register, the team considered putting a BFF in front of both systems, but chose to keep the STORES Register Rails app receiving requests and calling the STORES Reservations API as needed, so it could release early and watch customer reactions.
:::

:::fact
Changes in the data layer and middleware can be traced on the same blog. According to the post of 2023-10-26, the MongoDB behind the online shop and POS register runs on MongoDB Atlas and was upgraded to 5.0 ahead of the end of life of 4.4 in February 2024. According to a post of 2025-06-27, the session store of the Rails application behind the online shop was moved from MemoryDB (Redis OSS) to Valkey Serverless (ElastiCache Serverless), removing capacity management and moving to pay-as-you-go, at the cost of some unavailable commands such as KEYS and CLIENT LIST. Another post from the same day says plan subscriptions for the online shop and POS register were moved to Zuora between January and March 2025, unifying subscription management that each product had handled separately, and that a new cross-product plan structure was released on March 27. A third post from that day says a Go-based system built for integrating shop operations is being ported to Ruby on Rails, citing the growing gap between the company's Go and Ruby expertise and ecosystem investment, missing standard web framework features in the Go foundation, the inability to recoup the cost of running a separate server even if it saves memory, and latency and development inefficiency from API calls between systems.
:::

:::fact
According to the press release of 2026-07-17, as of May 2026 about 80% of the code at STORES is generated by AI, and an in-house AI agent named "kuro" supports development work. The company's own conference, STORES Tech Conf 2026 "World 2," held on August 31, took as its theme the shift to development methods that assume AI, with sessions including "Code generation supporting a large-scale admin screen migration by AI" and "Building the database of World 2, in World 2."
:::

:::guess
The overall shape appears to be "several monoliths built by different companies, connected through an in-house identity platform and API Gateway." The March 2026 move to one plan sits on the line that runs from the 2025 consolidation of subscriptions into Zuora and the port of the Go system to Rails, which suggests that aligning the billing machinery and the tech stack was a prerequisite for aligning the price list. The decision to move a Go foundation back to Rails reads less as a verdict on the languages than as a choice to match the company's majority stack and buy development speed. The claim that about 80% of code is AI-generated, and the conference session on code generation for a large admin screen migration, are presumably not unrelated to the volume of this porting work.
:::

## Business model

Revenue comes from two sources: the monthly fee and payment fees proportional to sales. The monthly fee has been simplified to either ¥0 or ¥3,300, while payment fees are finely split by channel and business size.

:::fact
According to the press release of 2026-03-26, from March 24, 2026 STORES abolished per-service contracts and moved fully to a structure in which a shop signs one plan and combines its eight main services, including cashless payments, POS register, online shop, and booking, as it likes. The Free and Standard plans, previously limited to small and medium businesses, were opened to businesses of every size, and on the Standard plan the in-person credit card fee starts at 1.98% for small and medium businesses and at 2.98% for larger ones (annual cashless payments of ¥30 million or more, or not qualifying as a small or medium business). Businesses using a single service would receive individual guidance on migration.
:::

:::fact
According to the pricing page (as of 2026-10-06), the Standard plan includes one payment terminal on free loan (shipped after passing the merchant review, returned on cancellation, additional units purchased), free replacement of broken terminals, discounts on in-person credit card fees, invoice card payment fees, and online shop payment fees, and free ordering of items in the POS register. Advanced features outside the package are added as single-product plans such as Reservations (Small, Team, Business) or Mobile Order, with the monthly total being the package plus the add-ons. The loyalty points and rank system costs from ¥22,000 a month with STORES integration and from ¥44,000 a month with Shopify integration, and branded apps are quoted individually.
:::

:::guess
For shops with small sales, ¥3,300 a month looks like the price of buying a fee discount. For the online shop, the gap between 5.5% on Free and 3.6% on Standard is 1.9 points, so the monthly fee pays for itself once monthly sales exceed roughly ¥170,000 (this site's estimate). For in-person payments the gap is only 0.5 points (2.48% versus 1.98%), but the ¥27,720 terminal becomes a free loan instead, so for a single shop the terminal alone offsets most of the first year's fee. The design hands out a free plan widely and moves only shops that start selling onto the paid one, and the per-shop application appears to work as a way to collect the monthly fee several times over from multi-location businesses.
:::

:::guess
The company history lists eight years between the 2018 holding company and the 2026 price unification. The "subscriptions managed separately by each product" and "separate login accounts" described on the official blog appear to be why one company kept several price lists for so long. Consolidating subscriptions into Zuora in 2025 and opening the plans to every size in 2026 put STORES on the same footing as the single-service free plan of [BASE](/en/articles/base) and the payments-first bundle of [Square](/en/articles/square). The ¥55,000 Omakase Start service and the early support for the food tax rate change look like attempts to differentiate outside the monthly fee and payment rates, by taking the shop's workload itself off its hands.
:::

Bundling separate services from separate companies into one price and one account took eight years, two Rails monoliths, a Go identity platform, and a migration of subscriptions to Zuora. The result is a simple choice between ¥0 and ¥3,300 a month, and a table of payment fees that differ by channel. The simple part fits on one page; the number that applies to your own shop is one page further on.
